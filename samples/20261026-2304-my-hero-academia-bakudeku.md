The training grounds were quieter than Izuku remembered. Most of the debris from the last battle had been cleared, but the scorched patches of earth remained, black against the grass that was trying to grow back. He sat on the low wall at the edge of the field, crutches propped beside him, watching the wind move the dust.

Katsuki found him there just after dusk.

“You’re not supposed to be out here,” Katsuki said. He stopped a few meters away, hands in the pockets of his hoodie. The sleeves were pushed up; the scars on his forearms were still pink.

“I needed air,” Izuku answered. His voice was thinner than it used to be. The doctors said the lung damage would improve. Eventually.

Katsuki didn’t argue. He sat on the wall too, leaving space between them. For a while neither of them spoke. Somewhere in the dorms a door slammed. Laughter drifted out, then faded.

“How’s the leg?” Katsuki asked.

“Still attached.”

“Funny.”

Izuku glanced sideways. Katsuki was looking at the field, not at him. The line of his jaw was tight.

“I heard you were cleared for light training,” Izuku said.

“Yeah.”

“Are you going to?”

Katsuki shrugged. The motion pulled at the scar across his shoulder. “Eventually.”

They sat until the lights on the main building switched on. Izuku’s hands were cold. He tucked them into his sleeves. Katsuki noticed.

“You’re freezing,” he said.

“I’m fine.”

“You’re not.” Katsuki stood. “Come on. I’ll walk you back.”

Izuku reached for the crutches. Katsuki didn’t offer to help, which was its own kind of consideration. He matched his pace to Izuku’s slow one. The path was uneven in places; Izuku watched the ground more than he watched Katsuki.

At the dorm entrance Katsuki held the door. Inside, the common room was mostly empty. Someone had left a blanket on the couch. Katsuki steered Izuku toward it without touching him.

“Sit.”

Izuku sat. His leg protested. He set the crutches aside and rubbed at his knee through the brace.

Katsuki disappeared into the kitchen. When he came back he had two mugs. One he set on the table in front of Izuku. The steam smelled like ginger.

“You don’t have to—”

“Shut up and drink it.”

Izuku drank. It was too hot, then just right. Katsuki sat on the other end of the couch, not quite looking at him.

“The others are going out tomorrow,” Katsuki said. “Some stupid dinner thing. You coming?”

“I don’t know.”

“You should.”

“Why?”

Katsuki’s mouth twitched. “Because if you stay here they’ll all start asking me how you are, and I don’t want to talk about it.”

Izuku almost smiled. “You could just tell them I’m fine.”

“You’re not fine.”

The words landed between them. Izuku stared at his mug.

“I’m getting better,” he said quietly.

“I know.” Katsuki’s voice was rough. “I can see that. Doesn’t mean you’re fine.”

They finished the tea. The common room stayed quiet. Outside, the wind picked up. Izuku’s eyes were heavy. He hadn’t slept well in weeks. Every time he closed them he saw the sky splitting open, heard the sound of something breaking that shouldn’t have broken.

Katsuki shifted. “You can go up if you want. I’ll lock up.”

“In a minute.”

They stayed. Izuku’s head tipped back against the cushion. He didn’t mean to doze, but the warmth of the mug and the low sound of Katsuki’s breathing pulled at him. When he opened his eyes again the lights had been dimmed. Katsuki was still there, scrolling through something on his phone with the brightness turned all the way down.

“Sorry,” Izuku said.

“For what?”

“Falling asleep.”

Katsuki put the phone away. “You look like shit. You should sleep more.”

“I try.”

“Try harder.”

Izuku huffed a laugh that turned into a cough. Katsuki’s hand twitched like he was going to reach over, then didn’t.

“Kacchan.”

“What.”

“Thank you. For the tea. And for… this.”

Katsuki’s ears went red. He looked at the floor. “Don’t make it weird.”

“I’m not.”

They were quiet again. Izuku could feel the space between them on the couch like something physical. He wanted to close it. He didn’t know how.

“I’m going to bed,” Katsuki said eventually. He stood. “You should too.”

“Okay.”

Katsuki hesitated at the bottom of the stairs. “If you can’t sleep,” he said, not turning around, “my door’s open. Not for anything. Just—if it’s bad.”

Izuku’s throat tightened. “Okay.”

Katsuki went up. Izuku stayed on the couch a little longer, listening to the building settle. When he finally stood, his leg was stiff. He climbed the stairs slowly. On the second-floor landing he paused outside Katsuki’s room. The door was ajar, a thin line of light under it. He didn’t go in. He went to his own room instead and lay down with the brace still on.

He didn’t sleep for a long time. When he did, the dreams were quieter.

The next evening the others left for dinner without them. Izuku had said he was tired. Katsuki had said he had work to finish. Neither of them had been convincing.

Izuku was on the couch again when Katsuki came down. He had a stack of papers under one arm and a frown.

“You’re still here,” Katsuki said.

“So are you.”

Katsuki dropped the papers on the table and sat. “The assignments are bullshit this week.”

“Want help?”

“No.”

He worked anyway. Izuku read. The only sounds were the turn of pages and the occasional scratch of Katsuki’s pen. After an hour Katsuki set the pen down and stretched. The movement made him wince.

“Shoulder?” Izuku asked.

“It’s fine.”

“You keep saying that.”

“Because it is.”

Izuku closed his book. “Let me see.”

Katsuki gave him a look. “What are you going to do, Deku? You’re on crutches.”

“I can still look.”

Katsuki rolled his eyes, but he pulled the hoodie off. Underneath he wore a thin tank top. The scar on his left shoulder was thick and raised, running from the collarbone toward the back. Izuku had seen it before, in the hospital, but not like this—under ordinary light, in the common room, with no one else around.

“It’s healing,” Izuku said.

“Yeah.”

“Does it hurt when you move?”

“Sometimes.”

Izuku reached out without thinking. His fingers brushed the edge of the scar. Katsuki went still. Izuku didn’t pull away.

“Sorry,” he said.

“Don’t be.”

Katsuki’s voice was low. Izuku looked up. Katsuki was watching him, expression unreadable. The space between them had narrowed somehow. Izuku’s hand was still on Katsuki’s shoulder. He could feel the heat of the skin, the slight give of the scar tissue.

“Kacchan,” he said.

“What.”

Izuku didn’t know what he wanted to say. The words were there, somewhere under his ribs, but they wouldn’t come out clean. He moved his thumb once, carefully, over the scar. Katsuki’s breath hitched.

“You don’t have to—” Katsuki started.

“I want to.”

They stayed like that. Izuku’s fingers traced the line of the scar to where it disappeared under the tank top. Katsuki didn’t stop him. Outside, a car passed. The headlights swept across the ceiling and vanished.

Katsuki’s hand came up and covered Izuku’s. Not pulling it away. Just holding it there.

“You’re an idiot,” he said quietly.

“I know.”

“I’m not good at this.”

“I know that too.”

Katsuki’s mouth twitched. Almost a smile. He leaned forward until their foreheads touched. Izuku closed his eyes. Katsuki’s skin was warm. His hand was steady around Izuku’s.

“If you tell anyone,” Katsuki said, “I’ll kill you.”

“I won’t.”

They sat like that until Izuku’s neck started to ache. When he shifted, Katsuki let go of his hand but didn’t move far. Their knees were touching now. Izuku could feel the solid line of Katsuki’s leg against his braced one.

“Stay,” Katsuki said.

“I’m not going anywhere.”

Katsuki exhaled. The sound was rough. He turned his head so his temple rested against Izuku’s. Neither of them spoke again for a long time.

Later, when the others came back, laughing and loud in the entryway, Katsuki had already gone upstairs. Izuku was still on the couch, book open on his lap, pretending to read. His shoulder felt warm where Katsuki’s head had been.

He didn’t tell anyone.

He didn’t need to.

The days after that were careful. They didn’t talk about it. Katsuki still walked Izuku back from the training grounds when he went. He still made tea. Sometimes he sat close enough that their shoulders brushed. Izuku didn’t move away. Katsuki didn’t either.

One afternoon Izuku’s physical therapist cleared him for short walks without the crutches. He went out alone first, testing the path around the dorms. The air was colder. His leg held. When he came back inside, Katsuki was in the kitchen, staring at the kettle like it had offended him.

“You walked,” Katsuki said.

“A little.”

“How was it?”

“Okay.”

Katsuki nodded. He poured water. Two mugs again. He set one in front of Izuku without asking.

They drank standing at the counter. Izuku’s hands were steadier than they had been a week ago. Katsuki watched them.

“You’re getting better,” he said.

“Yeah.”

“Good.”

Izuku looked at him. Katsuki’s ears were red again. He was staring into his mug.

“Kacchan.”

“What.”

“I like this,” Izuku said. “Being here. With you.”

Katsuki’s jaw worked. He set the mug down carefully.

“Yeah,” he said. “Me too.”

He reached across the counter. His fingers touched the back of Izuku’s hand, light, then firmer. Izuku turned his hand over so their palms met. Katsuki’s grip was warm and sure.

They stood like that until the kettle clicked off again, forgotten. Outside, the light was fading. Inside, the common room was quiet, and neither of them let go.

In the weeks that followed, the walks got longer. Katsuki started meeting him at the edge of the grounds without being asked. They didn’t talk much while they walked. Sometimes Katsuki pointed out a patch of ground that had started to green again. Sometimes Izuku stopped to rest and Katsuki waited without comment, hands in his pockets, eyes on the horizon.

One evening the wind was sharp enough that Izuku’s lungs protested. He slowed. Katsuki slowed with him.

“We can go back,” Katsuki said.

“In a minute.”

They stood at the far end of the path where the trees started. The light was thin. Izuku’s breath fogged. Katsuki shifted closer until their arms touched through the sleeves of their jackets.

“You don’t have to keep doing this,” Izuku said.

“Doing what.”

“Walking with me. Making tea. Sitting there when I can’t sleep.”

Katsuki was quiet for a moment. “I know.”

“Then why?”

Katsuki looked at the trees. His voice, when it came, was low. “Because I want to.”

Izuku turned. Katsuki met his eyes. The usual sharpness was still there, but under it something else had settled—something steady.

“Okay,” Izuku said.

Katsuki nodded once. They started back. Halfway to the dorms Katsuki’s hand found Izuku’s. The grip was careful around the places that still ached. Izuku held on.

They didn’t let go until they reached the door.
