The cabin sat at the end of a gravel road that had already started to freeze. Shane parked the rental and sat with the engine off, hands loose on the wheel. The lake was a flat grey sheet under a sky the same colour. He had not planned on coming. Yuna had forwarded the confirmation with a note that said the place was already paid for, that he needed somewhere quiet, that she would not ask questions if he did not answer them.

He carried his bag inside. The heat was on. Someone had left a note on the counter in careful block letters: *groceries in fridge, wood stacked, wifi password on the back*. Shane turned it over. No name. He set it down and opened the fridge. Milk, eggs, a paper-wrapped package of something that looked like smoked fish. He closed the door again.

He was still standing there when the second car pulled up.

Ilya did not knock. He walked in with a duffel and a paper bag of his own, stopped when he saw Shane, and set both bags down carefully.

"Hollander."

"Rozanov."

They looked at each other across the small kitchen. Outside, the wind moved through the bare trees. Shane watched Ilya’s shoulders settle under the jacket he had not taken off.

"Yuna," Shane said.

"Svetlana," Ilya said.

Neither of them smiled.

They did not speak much the first day. Ilya took the smaller bedroom without asking. Shane made eggs and left half on a plate. Ilya ate them standing at the counter and washed the pan. In the afternoon Shane walked the shoreline until his hands went numb. When he came back Ilya was on the porch with a mug, watching the ice form at the edges of the lake. He did not turn around when Shane climbed the steps.

"You are limping," Ilya said.

Shane stopped. "Old ankle."

"From the hit in November."

Shane did not answer. He went inside and ran the shower until the steam filled the small bathroom. When he came out Ilya had started a fire. The room smelled like pine and smoke. They sat at opposite ends of the couch and watched it catch. The silence was not comfortable. It was just quiet.

On the second day the temperature dropped. Shane’s ankle stiffened overnight. He sat on the edge of the bed and worked his sock on carefully. When he stood the joint clicked and held. He made it to the kitchen. Ilya was already there, coffee poured into two mugs. He pushed one across the table without looking up.

"Sit," he said.

Shane sat. Ilya crouched in front of him and took the ankle in both hands. His palms were warm. He pressed along the tendon with his thumbs, slow and methodical, the way a trainer would. Shane kept his eyes on the table.

"You should have stayed off it," Ilya said.

"I did."

"Not enough."

He wrapped it with a clean bandage from the first-aid kit under the sink. His fingers were steady. When he finished he stayed where he was, one hand still resting on Shane’s shin. The fire popped. Outside, ice cracked on the lake.

"I watched the replay," Ilya said. "You went down wrong."

"I know."

Ilya looked up. The light from the window caught the scar at the edge of his eyebrow, the one Shane had put there in their third year. Shane’s pulse kicked. He did not move his leg.

"You play like you are trying to disappear," Ilya said. "Even when you score."

Shane swallowed. "That’s not your problem."

"It is when I am the one who has to watch it."

He stood. Shane stayed where he was until the floorboards stopped creaking under Ilya’s feet.

They spent the afternoon on separate ends of the property. Shane tried to read and gave up. Ilya split wood until the pile by the door doubled. When the light started to go they met again in the kitchen. Ilya had the smoked fish out and was slicing bread. Shane took the knife without asking and finished the job. Their elbows brushed. Neither of them stepped back.

Dinner was quiet. After, Ilya washed and Shane dried. The radio on the counter played something low and instrumental. Shane set a plate in the rack and felt Ilya’s eyes on him.

"You have not said anything about the trade rumours," Ilya said.

"There are always trade rumours."

"Not like this."

Shane turned the towel over in his hands. "If they move me, they move me."

"You would hate Ottawa."

"I would hate a lot of places."

Ilya took the towel from him. Their fingers caught. He did not let go right away. The radio kept playing. Outside, the wind had stopped.

"I would hate it if you were somewhere I could not see you," Ilya said.

The words sat between them. Shane looked at their hands. Ilya’s thumb moved once, small, against the side of Shane’s wrist.

"You see me every month," Shane said.

"Not enough."

Shane pulled his hand back. He finished the dishes alone. When he turned around Ilya was still standing there, arms loose at his sides, watching him like he was waiting for something Shane did not know how to give.

On the third night the power went out.

They lit the candles from the drawer and sat on the floor in front of the fire. The room was warmer close to the hearth. Shane’s ankle ached less when he kept it elevated on a cushion. Ilya sat beside him, knees drawn up, one arm braced behind him. Their shoulders touched. Neither moved.

"You are shaking," Ilya said.

"Cold."

Ilya shifted. He put his arm around Shane’s shoulders and pulled him in until Shane’s head rested against the side of his neck. The position was awkward. Shane’s ear pressed against the collar of Ilya’s shirt. He could feel the steady beat under the fabric.

"Better," Ilya said.

Shane closed his eyes. The fire moved across the ceiling. Ilya’s hand rested between Shane’s shoulder blades, not stroking, just holding. They stayed like that until the candles burned low.

"I do not know how to do this," Shane said into the dark.

"Neither do I."

"We have been doing it for years."

Ilya’s hand tightened. "Not this."

Shane lifted his head. Ilya was looking at him, close enough that Shane could see the individual lashes, the small cut on his lower lip from a stick the week before. Shane reached up and touched it with his thumb. Ilya went still.

"Does it hurt," Shane asked.

"No."

Shane left his hand there. Ilya turned his face into the palm. His mouth brushed the center of Shane’s hand. The contact was brief. It stayed with Shane longer than the touch itself.

In the morning the power was back. They made coffee and sat at the table with the windows open to the cold air. Ilya’s knee rested against Shane’s under the table. Shane did not move it.

"Svetlana will ask," Ilya said.

"Yuna will too."

"What will you tell her."

Shane looked at the lake. The ice had thickened overnight. "That it was quiet."

Ilya nodded. He stood and took their mugs to the sink. When he came back he stopped beside Shane’s chair. His hand settled on the back of Shane’s neck, warm and certain. Shane leaned into it without thinking.

"Come to Boston in February," Ilya said. "After the game. Stay."

"Okay."

Ilya’s thumb moved once against the skin under Shane’s hair. "Okay."

They packed in the afternoon. Shane’s ankle held. Ilya carried both bags to the cars. At the edge of the gravel Shane stopped. Ilya turned.

"I watched the replay too," Shane said. "The one where you took the high stick for me in December."

Ilya’s mouth lifted at one corner. "I know."

Shane stepped in. He kissed him once, careful, on the uninjured side of his mouth. Ilya’s hand came up to the side of Shane’s face and stayed there. The kiss did not last long. When they pulled apart the air between them felt different.

"February," Shane said.

"February," Ilya said.

Shane drove away first. In the rearview mirror Ilya stood with his hands in his pockets, watching the car until the trees closed around it. Shane kept one hand on the wheel and the other on the gearshift, and did not turn the radio on until he reached the main road.
