The rain on the greenhouse glass was louder than either of them. Harry kept his hands in his pockets because if he took them out he would reach for the edge of Draco’s sleeve again, the way he had in the corridor after the last duel. Draco was cataloguing something on a scrap of parchment, the tip of his quill catching on the damp page.

“You’re bleeding,” Harry said.

Draco did not look up. “It’s nothing.”

It was not nothing. The cut along the heel of his palm was still open. Harry stepped closer anyway. The air between them smelled of wet earth and the sharp green of something Draco had been brewing. When Harry’s fingers closed around Draco’s wrist, Draco went still in the way that meant he had decided not to pull away.

“I can do it,” Harry said.

“I know.” Draco’s voice was low. He turned his hand over, palm up, and left it there. The rain kept falling. Harry did not let go.
