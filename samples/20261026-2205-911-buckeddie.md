The kitchen light was the only one on when Buck let himself in. Eddie’s house smelled like the tomato sauce he’d started before the shift, and Christopher’s backpack sat by the door, unzipped, a math worksheet half-stuffed inside. Buck closed the door carefully behind him and stood still for a second, listening. No television. No footsteps.

“Eds?”

“Kitchen.”

Eddie was at the stove, stirring. His shoulders were set the way they got after a call that didn’t go clean. He didn’t turn around. “You stayed past shift.”

“Hen needed the extra hands on the paperwork. And you left without saying anything.”

Eddie set the spoon down. The sauce bubbled once and settled. “Chris was with Abuela. I wanted to get him home.”

“I know.” Buck moved to the counter and leaned on it, careful of his ribs. The harness had caught him across the chest when the beam shifted. Nothing broken, the paramedic said. Just bruised deep enough that breathing felt like a decision. “You okay?”

“I’m fine.” Eddie finally looked at him. His eyes tracked the way Buck was holding himself. “You’re not.”

“It’s nothing.”

“Sit down.”

Buck sat. Eddie turned the burner off, covered the pot, and came around the island. He didn’t touch him right away. He stood close enough that Buck could smell the smoke still caught in his hair.

“Shirt off.”

“Eddie—”

“I want to see it.”

Buck pulled the LAFD tee over his head. The bruise had already gone purple across his left side, high under the arm and spreading toward the sternum. Eddie’s mouth went tight. He didn’t say anything. He just looked, then went to the freezer and came back with an ice pack wrapped in a dish towel.

“Here.”

Buck pressed it to the worst of it. The cold made his breath catch. Eddie stayed standing, one hand on the back of the chair.

“You stepped in front of me,” Eddie said.

“The beam was coming down. You were already under the other one.”

“I had it.”

“You didn’t.” Buck adjusted the ice pack. The kitchen was quiet except for the tick of the cooling stove. “You would’ve taken it across the shoulders. Chris needs you walking.”

Eddie was quiet long enough that Buck looked up. Eddie was watching his face, not the bruise.

“You think I don’t need you walking?”

Buck didn’t answer. The ice was starting to numb the skin. He could feel the heat of Eddie’s hand still resting on the chair back, close enough that if he leaned sideways their arms would touch.

Christopher’s voice came from the hallway. “Dad? Is Buck staying?”

Eddie didn’t look away from Buck when he answered. “Yeah. He’s staying.”

They ate at the table. Christopher talked about the science fair project and whether the volcano would actually erupt or just spill. Buck kept the ice pack against his side under the table and answered when he was supposed to. Eddie watched both of them. When Christopher went to brush his teeth, Eddie cleared the plates and Buck stayed where he was, listening to the water run.

Eddie came back with two beers and set one in front of Buck.

“You should take something for that.”

“I did. In the truck.”

Eddie sat. He turned the bottle between his hands. “I keep thinking about what happens if the next one is worse.”

“Eddie.”

“You have a key. You have the will. You pick him up from school when I can’t. And I still don’t know how to say the rest of it.”

Buck set the beer down. The bottle left a ring on the table. “Say what?”

Eddie looked at the hallway, then back. His voice was low. “That I want you here when the shift is over. Not just when something’s wrong. That I look for you before I look for anyone else. That when you step in front of a beam I can’t breathe until I know you’re standing up.”

The ice pack had slipped. Buck let it. His ribs ached in a steady, ordinary way. He could hear Christopher moving in the bathroom, the sink running again.

“I’ve been trying not to want that,” Buck said.

“Why?”

“Because if I say it out loud and you don’t—if it’s just me—”

“It’s not just you.” Eddie’s hand was flat on the table between them. Not reaching. Just there. “I’ve been waiting for you to notice I was already looking.”

Buck stared at the hand. The kitchen light made the scars on Eddie’s knuckles stand out. He put his own hand next to it, close enough that their pinkies touched. Neither of them moved for a long moment.

Christopher’s door closed. The house settled.

Eddie turned his hand over. Buck’s fingers fit against his palm without either of them having to decide it. The contact was warm and ordinary and nothing like ordinary at all.

“Stay,” Eddie said. “Not just tonight.”

Buck’s thumb moved once across the back of Eddie’s hand. “Okay.”

They sat like that until the ice pack melted and the beers went warm. Outside, a car passed. Inside, Christopher’s nightlight glowed under the edge of his door. Eddie’s thumb rested against Buck’s wrist, counting the pulse there the way he counted respirations on a call. Steady. Present.

Buck leaned his shoulder against Eddie’s. The bruise protested. He stayed anyway.

“Tomorrow,” Eddie said, quiet, “we figure out the rest.”

“Yeah.” Buck closed his eyes for a second. The kitchen smelled like tomato sauce and the soap Christopher used. Eddie’s shoulder was solid under his. “Tomorrow.”

They didn’t move until the light under Christopher’s door went out. Then Eddie stood, kept hold of Buck’s hand, and turned off the kitchen light on the way to the couch. The house went dark except for the streetlight through the blinds. Buck settled carefully against the cushions. Eddie sat beside him, close enough that their legs touched from knee to ankle.

Neither of them spoke. They didn’t need to. The day had already said the part that mattered.

Buck’s ribs still hurt when he breathed. Eddie’s hand was still around his. Outside, the street was quiet. Inside, the house held them both, and Christopher sleeping down the hall, and the ordinary fact that Buck would still be there in the morning.

He closed his eyes. Eddie’s thumb moved once more against his wrist, then stayed. The pulse under it kept time with the low hum of the refrigerator and the slow, careful breath Buck took against the ache in his side.

It was enough. It was more than enough.

For a long time they stayed like that, the television dark, the remote untouched on the coffee table. Eddie’s free hand rested on his own knee. Buck could feel the warmth of it even without looking. When he shifted, the bruise pulled, and Eddie’s fingers tightened once around his wrist, not hard, just enough to say he noticed.

“You don’t have to stay on the couch,” Eddie said eventually.

“I know.”

“Chris has school. I’ll be up early.”

“I know that too.”

Eddie was quiet. His thumb had stopped moving. Buck opened his eyes. In the dark he could make out the line of Eddie’s shoulder, the way his head was tilted toward the hallway like he was listening for Christopher even now.

“I don’t want to wake him,” Buck said.

“You won’t.” Eddie’s voice was low. “He sleeps through the alarm most days. And he already knows you’re here.”

Buck turned his head. Their faces were close enough that he could see the faint shine of Eddie’s eyes. “Does he?”

“He asked if you were staying before he even saw the bruise.” Eddie’s mouth moved, almost a smile. “He doesn’t miss much.”

Buck let out a breath that hurt a little. “He’s a good kid.”

“Yeah.” Eddie’s fingers loosened, then settled again. “He is.”

They sat with that. The refrigerator clicked off. Somewhere outside a dog barked once and stopped. Buck’s free hand rested on his own thigh. He could feel the edge of the bruise when he inhaled. He thought about the beam, the sound it made when it shifted, the way Eddie’s face had gone blank for half a second before Buck moved. He thought about the will, the key, the afternoons he’d spent at the kitchen table helping with homework while Eddie finished a late shift. He thought about all the times he’d stood in this kitchen and wanted to stay and hadn’t said it.

“I’ve been scared,” he said.

Eddie didn’t ask of what. He just waited.

“That if I said it, it would change what we already have. That you’d have to choose between me and the way things are with Chris. That I’d mess it up.”

Eddie’s hand turned under his. Their palms pressed together. “You already chose,” he said. “When you put your name on the paper. When you stepped under that beam. I just needed to hear you say the rest out loud so I could stop pretending I wasn’t waiting for it.”

Buck’s throat was tight. He swallowed. “I want to be here. Not just when you’re working. Not just when something breaks. I want the mornings. I want to know what he has for breakfast and whether the volcano actually works. I want—” He stopped. The words felt too large for the dark room. “I want you.”

Eddie’s breath left him in a quiet rush. His free hand came up, slow, and rested against the side of Buck’s neck. His thumb settled just under the jaw. The touch was careful, the same way he’d handled the ice pack earlier, like he was checking for damage and finding none that mattered.

“Then stay,” he said again. Softer this time. “We’ll figure out the rest tomorrow. And the day after that.”

Buck leaned into the hand. His ribs pulled. He didn’t care. Eddie’s forehead touched his, light, and they stayed like that, breathing the same small space of air. Outside, another car went by. Inside, Christopher slept. The house was quiet around them, ordinary and full.

When Eddie finally stood, he kept hold of Buck’s hand and led him down the hall past Christopher’s door. He didn’t turn on the light in the bedroom. He just pulled the covers back on the side of the bed that wasn’t his and waited while Buck eased himself down, careful of the bruise. Eddie sat on the edge for a moment, then lay down beside him, close but not crowding. His hand found Buck’s again under the blanket.

Buck listened to the sound of Eddie’s breathing even out. His own ribs still ached, a steady reminder of the day. He turned his head just enough that his temple rested against Eddie’s shoulder. Eddie’s fingers tightened once, then relaxed.

In the morning Christopher would wake up and find Buck still there. They would make breakfast. The volcano project would need more baking soda. Eddie would go back to the station and Buck would follow him after his ribs stopped complaining quite so loudly. None of it would be simple. All of it would be theirs.

Buck closed his eyes. Eddie’s thumb moved once more against his wrist, counting the pulse the way he always did, and then stayed. Outside, the street stayed quiet. Inside, the house held them both.

It was enough. It was more than enough.
