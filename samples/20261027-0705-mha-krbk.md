The apartment still smelled like the agency laundry soap. Kirishima noticed it every time he shifted on the couch and the blanket moved. Outside the window the city was dark except for the red lights on the construction cranes two blocks over. His left arm was in a sling. The cast stopped just below the elbow. Under it the skin itched where the hardening quirk had cracked and reformed wrong during the collapse.

Bakugou was in the kitchen. The tap ran. A cabinet opened and closed. Kirishima kept his eyes on the ceiling.

“You ate?” Bakugou’s voice carried without effort.

“Earlier.”

“When.”

“Before you left for the debrief.”

A plate hit the low table in front of the couch harder than necessary. Rice, grilled fish, sliced cucumber. The cucumber was cut the way Kirishima liked, thin and cold. Bakugou set a glass of water beside it and stayed standing.

“Eat it while it’s hot.”

Kirishima sat up. The movement pulled at the stitches along his ribs. He breathed through it and reached for the chopsticks with his right hand. Bakugou watched until the first bite was in his mouth, then turned away and started washing the pan he had used.

They had been doing this for eleven days. The agency had released Kirishima to home recovery after the second surgery. Bakugou had a key. He used it. Most nights he stayed until the dishes were done and the pain medication was taken. Some nights he left after that. Some nights he stayed longer and pretended the reason was the early patrol the next morning.

Kirishima finished half the fish before the nausea hit. He set the chopsticks down. Bakugou did not turn around.

“Don’t force it.”

“I’m not.”

“You’re making that face.”

“What face.”

“The one that means you’re gonna hurl and then pretend you didn’t.”

Kirishima laughed once. It hurt. He drank the water instead. The glass was cold against his palm. Bakugou dried his hands on a towel and finally looked at him.

The swelling around Kirishima’s left eye had gone down. The cut on his cheek was a thin red line. Bakugou’s gaze stopped there, then moved to the sling, then to the way Kirishima was holding his ribs without meaning to.

“Physical therapy tomorrow,” Bakugou said.

“I know.”

“I’ll drive.”

“You have a shift.”

“I switched.”

Kirishima nodded. He did not ask how. Bakugou never offered the details. He just appeared with the car keys and a paper bag of the protein bars the therapist said were fine.

The television was on low. A news recap of the same building collapse they had already lived through. Kirishima reached for the remote and turned it off. The silence was better. Bakugou sat on the other end of the couch, close enough that their knees almost touched when Kirishima stretched his legs out. Neither of them moved away.

Kirishima’s phone buzzed. A message from Mina about the group chat. He left it. Bakugou’s phone stayed face down on the table. His hand rested on his own thigh, fingers tapping once, then still.

“You should sleep,” Bakugou said after a while.

“In a minute.”

“Your minute is twenty.”

“I know how long a minute is.”

Bakugou made a sound that was almost a laugh. He stood, collected the plate, and took it to the kitchen. When he came back he had the spare blanket from the closet. He dropped it over Kirishima’s legs without asking. The weight settled. Kirishima’s hand found the edge of it and held on.

“Thanks,” he said.

Bakugou sat again. This time he left less space. His shoulder touched Kirishima’s good arm. The contact was warm through the thin t-shirt. Neither of them spoke. The city noise outside was steady. A car passed. Somewhere a dog barked once and stopped.

Kirishima’s eyes closed. He did not mean for them to. The pain medication made the edges of everything soft. He felt Bakugou shift, careful, and then the low sound of the television volume being turned down to nothing. A hand settled on his knee over the blanket. It stayed there. Kirishima breathed in and out. The hand did not move.

He woke later with his head against Bakugou’s shoulder. The room was dark except for the light under the kitchen cabinet. Bakugou was awake. His breathing was even. His hand was still on Kirishima’s knee. When Kirishima lifted his head, Bakugou looked at him.

“Go to bed,” Bakugou said. His voice was quiet.

“You too.”

“I’m fine here.”

Kirishima stood. The room tilted and settled. He walked to the bedroom. At the door he stopped and looked back. Bakugou was watching the dark window. His profile was sharp in the low light.

“Katsuki.”

Bakugou turned his head.

“Stay,” Kirishima said.

Bakugou was quiet for three seconds. Then he stood and followed.

They slept on top of the covers. Kirishima on his back, Bakugou on his side facing him. Their hands met between them sometime after midnight. Neither of them let go.

Morning light came through the blinds in thin lines. Kirishima’s ribs ached the way they always did when he first moved. Bakugou was already up. The smell of coffee reached the bedroom. Kirishima found him at the counter in yesterday’s shirt, hair still flattened on one side.

“Therapy at nine,” Bakugou said without turning.

“I remember.”

Bakugou poured a second cup and set it where Kirishima could reach it. Their fingers brushed when Kirishima took it. Neither of them pulled away first.

On the drive, Bakugou kept one hand on the wheel and the other on the gear shift. Kirishima watched the buildings pass. At a red light Bakugou’s thumb tapped the leather once, twice.

“You can tell them if it hurts,” he said.

“I will.”

“Don’t do the thing where you smile and say you’re fine.”

“I won’t.”

The light changed. Bakugou drove. Kirishima looked at the side of his face and did not look away until they pulled into the clinic lot.

The session was short. The therapist moved Kirishima’s arm through small ranges and wrote numbers on a chart. Bakugou stood against the wall with his arms crossed and did not interrupt. When they left, Kirishima’s shirt stuck to his back with sweat. Bakugou opened the passenger door and waited until he was seated before closing it.

They stopped for food on the way back. Kirishima managed more of it than the night before. Bakugou ate standing at the counter in the small kitchen and watched him finish. When the plates were clear he washed them. Kirishima dried. Their elbows knocked once. Bakugou did not step aside.

That night the pain woke Kirishima at two. He sat on the edge of the bed and breathed through it. Bakugou’s hand found his back without either of them turning on the light.

“Scale of one to ten,” Bakugou said.

“Six.”

“Liar.”

“Seven.”

Bakugou’s palm stayed between his shoulder blades until the worst of it passed. Then he stood, went to the kitchen, and came back with water and the next dose. Kirishima took both. Bakugou sat beside him on the mattress until the numbers on the clock changed and the pain dropped enough that lying down was possible again.

They did not talk about it in the morning. Bakugou made eggs. Kirishima ate them. At the door, before Bakugou left for his shift, Kirishima caught his wrist.

“Come back after,” he said.

Bakugou looked at the hand on his wrist, then at Kirishima’s face. He nodded once.

The pattern held for another week. Bakugou’s shifts. Kirishima’s appointments. The spare key staying on Bakugou’s ring. Small touches that lasted longer each time. A hand on the back of the neck when Kirishima stood too fast. A shoulder pressed close on the couch while a movie neither of them watched played. Kirishima’s fingers in the hem of Bakugou’s shirt when the pain spiked and he needed something to hold.

On the fourteenth night Bakugou came in later than usual. There was a cut across his knuckles that had already scabbed. He washed it at the sink without comment. Kirishima watched from the couch.

“Bad one?” he asked.

“Nothing that needed a hospital.”

Bakugou dried his hands. He crossed the room and stopped in front of Kirishima. For a moment he only looked. Then he sat, close, and rested his forehead against Kirishima’s good shoulder. The weight of him was solid. Kirishima’s hand came up and settled in his hair. Bakugou’s breath left him in a long exhale.

“I’m fine,” Bakugou said into the fabric of the shirt.

“I know.”

They stayed like that until Kirishima’s arm started to ache from the angle. Bakugou lifted his head. Their faces were close. Kirishima could see the flecks of ash still caught in Bakugou’s eyelashes from whatever fight he had finished.

“Eijirou.”

“Yeah.”

Bakugou’s hand found the side of Kirishima’s neck. His thumb rested just under the jaw. The touch was careful. Kirishima leaned into it. Their mouths met once, brief, and then again, longer. Bakugou’s other hand braced on the couch so he did not put weight on the injured side. Kirishima’s fingers stayed in his hair.

When they separated, Bakugou’s forehead rested against his again.

“Okay?” Bakugou asked.

“Yeah.”

They kissed once more before Bakugou stood and went to lock the door. He came back. Kirishima made room. They lay down on the couch under the spare blanket, Bakugou’s arm across Kirishima’s waist, careful of the ribs. Outside, the cranes blinked red against the dark. Inside, the only sound was their breathing, steady and close.

In the morning Bakugou’s phone alarm went off at six. He silenced it without opening his eyes. Kirishima’s hand was already on his chest. Neither of them moved for a long time.

“Therapy at nine,” Bakugou said eventually.

“I know.”

“I’ll drive.”

Kirishima smiled against his shoulder. The cast itched. His ribs still pulled. Bakugou’s heartbeat was steady under his palm. He closed his eyes again and let the light through the blinds warm the side of his face.

They had time.
