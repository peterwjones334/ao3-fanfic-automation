The trailer sat low against the tree line at the far end of the park. Eddie’s van was parked crooked in the space beside it, one tire half on the gravel. The screen door stuck on the frame when Steve pushed it open. Inside the light was yellow from the single lamp and the air held the smell of the coffee Eddie had left on the stove and the cigarette he had put out an hour ago.

Steve set the paper bag on the counter. The plastic handles had cut into his fingers on the walk from the car. He had stopped at the pharmacy after closing Family Video. The bag held gauze, tape, and the ointment the nurse had said Eddie would need if the cut opened again.

Eddie sat on the couch with his left arm propped on a pillow. The bandage ran from wrist to elbow. The edges were already dark at one corner. He looked up when Steve came in. His hair was half out of the ponytail; the ends stuck to his neck where the sweat had dried.

“Didn’t have to,” Eddie said.

“You said the same thing yesterday.”

“Still true.”

Steve unpacked the bag. The gauze unrolled clean against the counter. He set the bottle beside it. Eddie watched his hands. The television was on low, some show about cars that neither of them had chosen. The sound stayed under the refrigerator’s hum.

“How’s the arm,” Steve said.

“Itches.”

“Means it’s healing.”

Eddie flexed his fingers once. The movement pulled at the bandage. He stopped. “You work until close again tomorrow.”

“Yeah.”

“I can handle it.”

Steve looked at the arm. The doctor had said no lifting for a week. Eddie had tried to lift a crate of records the day after the stitches and the cut had opened across the middle. Steve had driven him back to the clinic with a towel under the arm and the radio off.

“You handled it last time by bleeding on the floor,” Steve said.

Eddie almost smiled. It did not reach his eyes. “Point.”

They ate sandwiches at the small table. Eddie used his right hand. Crumbs stayed on the plate. Steve cleared both when they finished and ran the water hot. Outside a dog barked once and stopped. The trees moved in a wind that did not reach the trailer.

After, Eddie sat on the edge of the bathtub while Steve changed the bandage. The light above the mirror was bright and made the skin around the cut look pink. No heat. No red lines running toward the elbow. Steve’s hands were careful with the tape. Eddie’s eyes stayed on the wall above the towel rack.

“Looks better,” Steve said.

“Feels better when you’re not staring at it.”

Steve’s thumb rested on the inside of Eddie’s wrist. The pulse was steady under the skin. He did not move his hand. Eddie’s breath stayed even. The mirror fogged at the edges from the shower Eddie had taken before Steve arrived.

“You don’t have to stay,” Eddie said. The words were quiet.

“I know.”

Steve smoothed the last strip of tape. His fingers lingered on the edge of the bandage. Eddie turned his head. Their eyes met in the mirror. Steve looked away first. He washed his hands at the sink. The soap smelled like the one Eddie kept under the counter, cheap and sharp.

In the living room Steve took the far end of the couch. Eddie stayed where he was. The space between them held the pillow and the remote. Eddie’s good hand rested on his thigh. Steve watched the way the fingers twitched when the car show changed to a commercial for tires.

“Robin asked about you,” Steve said.

“Tell her I’m not dead.”

“I did.”

Eddie nodded. He shifted. The movement brought his knee closer to Steve’s. Neither of them moved it back. The television light moved across Eddie’s face. The scar on his jaw from last year caught it and went dark again.

“You could crash here,” Eddie said. “Couch is free.”

“I’ve got the early shift.”

“So leave early.”

Steve looked at the couch. The cushions were worn. One of them had a tear Eddie had taped with black electrical tape. “Maybe.”

They sat until the show ended. Eddie’s eyes closed halfway. Steve turned the volume down until it was only the low murmur of the announcer. Outside the wind moved the trees. The trailer creaked once on its blocks. Eddie’s head tipped toward the pillow. Steve stayed where he was until the light outside the window went blue and the dog across the way settled.

In the morning Eddie was already awake. The coffee pot was on. Steve’s jacket hung over the back of the chair. Eddie stood at the counter with his arm held close to his side. He poured two mugs. The steam rose between them. Steve took the second one. Their fingers touched on the handle. Eddie did not pull away.

“Bandage needs changing before you go,” he said.

“I know.”

They stood in the kitchen. The light from the window cut across the counter and left the rest of the room in shadow. Eddie’s hair was still loose. Steve’s hand stayed on the mug longer than it needed to. Later, in the bathroom, with the tape already half off and the cut clean under the bright light, the pulse under Eddie’s wrist was still steady when Steve checked it.

The gate to the park was open when Steve left. Eddie watched from the step. The dog across the way barked once. Steve raised a hand from the driver’s seat. Eddie lifted his good one. The motion pulled at the bandage. He lowered it slow. The van stayed parked. The screen door stuck when Eddie closed it behind him.

Steve came back the next night with more gauze and a bag of the chips Eddie liked. The cut had not opened. The pink had faded to a thin line. Eddie let him change the bandage without sitting on the tub edge this time. He stood at the sink instead and watched in the mirror while Steve worked. Their shoulders were close enough that the fabric of Steve’s shirt brushed Eddie’s good arm.

“You keep showing up,” Eddie said.

“You keep needing the gauze.”

“Not tonight.”

Steve finished the tape. His hand stayed on Eddie’s wrist. The pulse jumped once under his thumb and settled. Eddie turned. The space between them was the width of the sink. Steve did not step back. Eddie’s eyes moved over his face and stopped at his mouth, then came back up.

“Still,” Eddie said.

Steve nodded. He let his hand drop. It did not go far. Outside the trees moved. Inside the refrigerator hummed. Eddie reached past him for the mug Steve had left on the counter. Their arms crossed. Neither of them moved until the coffee had gone cold.

They ate the chips on the couch. Eddie sat closer this time. His knee rested against Steve’s. The television stayed off. The only light came from the lamp and the small bulb above the stove. Eddie’s hair was tied back again. A few strands had come loose at the temple. Steve watched them and did not reach.

“You can stay,” Eddie said. “If you want. I won’t make you leave early.”

“I know.”

Steve stayed. He took the couch. Eddie took the bedroom and left the door open. In the dark the trailer settled. Steve listened to the sound of Eddie shifting on the mattress and the low creak of the frame. Neither of them spoke. In the morning the coffee was already poured and Eddie’s arm was bare under the rolled sleeve of his shirt. The line of the cut was clean. Steve touched the edge of it once, light, and Eddie let him.

The van stayed parked. The screen door stuck less when they both pushed it. Outside the dog barked and stopped. Inside the pulse under Eddie’s wrist stayed steady under Steve’s thumb.
