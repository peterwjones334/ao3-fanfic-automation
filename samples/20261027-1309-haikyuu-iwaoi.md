The gym lights in Tokyo hummed the same way they always had. Iwaizumi stood under them with a clipboard, watching the university team finish their cool-down. His knee clicked when he shifted his weight. Old habit, old injury. He ignored it.

His phone buzzed. The message was short.

*Flight lands at 18:40. Don't be late, Iwa-chan.*

He stared at it for a full minute before he typed back.

*I'm never late.*

Oikawa's reply was a single emoji, the one that meant he was already laughing.

Iwaizumi arrived at the airport twenty minutes early. He bought coffee he did not drink and watched the board update. When Oikawa finally walked out of customs, the first thing Iwaizumi noticed was the way he moved—still light on his feet, but careful with his left side. A wrap showed under the hem of his shirt when he lifted his bag.

"You look like shit," Iwaizumi said.

Oikawa's smile was automatic and bright. "You look the same. It's comforting."

They did not hug. They never had, not in public. Iwaizumi took the bag. Oikawa let him.

The drive was mostly quiet. Oikawa talked about the flight, the turbulence over the Andes, the teammate who had lent him a sweater that still smelled like laundry detergent from Buenos Aires. Iwaizumi listened and kept his eyes on the road. When they stopped at a light, Oikawa's hand rested on the center console, close enough that their knuckles almost touched. Neither of them moved it.

At the apartment, Oikawa kicked off his shoes and stood in the middle of the small living room like he was measuring it.

"You live here?"

"For now. Work assignment."

"Still fixing other people's bodies."

"Someone has to."

Oikawa laughed, short. He sat on the edge of the couch and rolled his shoulder carefully. Iwaizumi watched the motion.

"How bad is it?"

"Not bad. Strain. They told me to rest. I told them I had a reason to come home."

Iwaizumi set the bag down. "You didn't have to."

"I know."

They ate convenience store onigiri because neither of them wanted to cook. Oikawa complained that the rice was too soft. He finished three of them anyway. Later, Iwaizumi found him on the balcony with the ice pack from the freezer, knee elevated on a plastic crate.

"You could have asked," Iwaizumi said.

"I'm asking now."

Iwaizumi took the pack and repositioned it. His fingers were steady. Oikawa's were not. The city noise rose around them—trains, distant voices, the low thrum of traffic. Neither spoke for a long time.

"I almost didn't get on the plane," Oikawa said finally. "Thought maybe it would be easier if I just stayed where I was."

"Why didn't you?"

Oikawa looked at him. The balcony light caught the edge of his face, the line of his mouth that had always been a little too quick to smile.

"Because you answered."

The next morning they ran. Oikawa kept the pace easy. Iwaizumi matched him without comment. When they stopped at the river, Oikawa bent with his hands on his knees and breathed. Iwaizumi watched the rise and fall of his back and did not say anything about the way the shirt stuck to the line of his spine.

At breakfast, Oikawa stole the last piece of grilled fish from Iwaizumi's plate. Iwaizumi let him.

On the third day, Iwaizumi took him to the university gym after hours. The team had already left. The nets were still up. Oikawa stood at the service line and spun a ball in his hands the way he always had, fingers sure.

"One set," he said.

"You're supposed to be resting."

"One."

Iwaizumi went to the other side. The first toss was high and clean. He spiked it without thinking. The sound of the ball hitting the floor echoed. Oikawa set again. And again. They did not keep score. When Oikawa's form started to slip, Iwaizumi called it.

They sat on the floor with their backs against the wall. Sweat cooled on their skin. Oikawa's shoulder brushed Iwaizumi's when he shifted.

"I used to think if I got far enough away, it would stop mattering," Oikawa said. His voice was low. "That I'd stop looking for you in every gym."

Iwaizumi's hands rested on his knees. "Did it work?"

"No."

The silence stretched. Iwaizumi could hear Oikawa breathing. He could hear his own.

"I looked too," he said. "Every time the national team played. Every highlight. I told myself it was professional interest."

Oikawa turned his head. Their faces were close. "Was it?"

"No."

The kiss was slow because they made it slow. Oikawa's hand came up to the side of Iwaizumi's jaw, thumb resting just under the ear. Iwaizumi's fingers found the hem of Oikawa's shirt and stayed there, not pushing, just holding. When they pulled apart, Oikawa's forehead rested against his.

"Took us long enough," Oikawa said.

"Yeah."

They walked back to the apartment without talking. The streetlights came on one by one. Oikawa's limp was more noticeable now that the adrenaline had faded. Iwaizumi slowed his steps to match. At the corner store he bought two bottles of sports drink without asking. Oikawa drank half of his before they reached the building.

In the small kitchen, Iwaizumi made tea because his hands needed something to do. Oikawa stood close enough that their shoulders touched. When the kettle clicked off, neither of them moved right away. The steam fogged the window above the sink. Iwaizumi poured two cups. He added the sugar Oikawa still took even though he claimed he had stopped. Oikawa watched the spoon move and did not comment.

Later, on the couch, Oikawa's head was on Iwaizumi's shoulder. The television played something neither of them watched. Iwaizumi's hand rested on Oikawa's wrist, thumb moving once, slow, over the pulse point.

"What happens when you go back?" Iwaizumi asked.

"I don't know yet." Oikawa's voice was quiet. "But I'm not pretending this didn't happen."

"Good."

"You could visit."

"I could."

Oikawa lifted his head. His eyes were clear. "I want you to."

Iwaizumi nodded. The answer was already there.

The week passed in small pieces that accumulated weight. Mornings that started later, with Oikawa still half-asleep and Iwaizumi already measuring coffee. Meals that stretched because neither of them wanted to clear the table. One afternoon they took the train to the coast because Oikawa said he wanted to see the water. The car was crowded. Oikawa stood with his hand braced on the overhead rail, his other hand resting lightly on Iwaizumi's shoulder for balance when the train swayed. They stood on the platform and watched the waves. Oikawa's hand found Iwaizumi's and stayed. No one looked twice.

That night Oikawa's knee stiffened. He sat on the edge of the bed and tried to stretch it without making a sound. Iwaizumi heard him anyway. He came in with the ice pack and the elastic wrap he had bought that morning without saying why. He knelt and worked the wrap into place, fingers sure from years of doing the same for other people. Oikawa's hand settled on the back of his neck, not pulling, just resting.

"Hurts?" Iwaizumi asked.

"Not as much as it did on the plane."

"You should have said something then."

"I was busy not thinking about you."

Iwaizumi looked up. Oikawa's mouth was soft at the corners. The room was quiet except for the low sound of the air conditioner. Iwaizumi finished the wrap and stayed where he was, one hand still on Oikawa's calf. The muscle under his palm was warm. Oikawa's fingers moved once through the short hair at the nape of his neck.

"Stay," Oikawa said.

Iwaizumi stayed.

On the last night, they lay in the narrow bed with the window open. The city sounded far away. Oikawa traced the line of Iwaizumi's collarbone with one finger, careful.

"I was scared you'd moved on," he said.

"I tried."

"Me too."

Iwaizumi turned his head. Their noses almost touched. "Didn't work."

"No."

They kissed again, slower than the first time. Oikawa's knee rested against Iwaizumi's thigh. The ice pack sat forgotten on the nightstand. Outside, a train passed and the sound rose and faded. Neither of them moved to turn off the light.

In the morning, Iwaizumi drove him to the airport. They stood at the gate longer than necessary. Oikawa's bag was already checked. His boarding pass was in his hand.

"Call when you land," Iwaizumi said.

"I will."

Oikawa kissed him once, quick, then again slower. When he pulled back, his smile was smaller than usual and more real.

"See you soon, Iwa-chan."

"Yeah."

Iwaizumi watched him go until the gate swallowed him. On the drive home, the car felt larger. His phone stayed quiet until he was almost back at the apartment. The message was a photo of an airplane wing against blue sky, and underneath it, two words.

*Miss you already.*

Iwaizumi pulled over. He typed back with steady hands.

*Same.*

He sat there for a minute with the engine running, then put the phone down and drove the rest of the way. The apartment was quiet when he opened the door. On the kitchen counter, Oikawa had left the spare key and a note written on the back of a receipt.

*Don't lose this. I'll need it when I come back.*

Iwaizumi folded the note and put it in his wallet. Outside, the city kept moving. He made coffee he did not drink and stood at the window until the light changed. His phone buzzed again. Another message. He answered it.

The rest would come later. For now, this was enough.
