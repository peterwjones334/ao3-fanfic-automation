The apartment was smaller than Steve remembered from the one time Dustin had described it. Eddie sat on the edge of the couch with his left leg propped on a milk crate, the scar from the demobat bites still raised and pink along his calf. He didn't stand when Steve knocked. Just called out that the door was open.

Steve set the bag of takeout on the counter. Chinese, because Eddie had once said, half-joking in the van, that he liked the way the noodles stuck together when they got cold. "Dustin said you weren't answering his calls."

"Phone's dead." Eddie nodded at the charger cord coiled on the floor. "Forgot to plug it in. Again."

Steve plugged it in. The screen lit up with three missed calls and a text from Dustin that said *please just tell me you're not dead, man.* He turned the phone so Eddie could see it. Eddie looked at it for a second, then looked away.

"You drove four hours because the kid was worried."

"I drove four hours because I was worried." Steve opened the bag. The smell of soy sauce and fried rice filled the room. He found two plates that were mostly clean and started portioning food without asking. Eddie watched him.

They ate on the couch. Eddie's chopsticks were awkward with the brace still on his wrist from the fall off the trailer roof. Steve didn't offer to help. He just waited when Eddie had to set them down and use his fingers for a minute, then went back to eating when Eddie picked them up again.

"How's the leg," Steve said after a while.

"Stiff. Hurts when it rains." Eddie shrugged. "Doctor says it'll loosen up if I keep walking on it. So I walk."

"Where."

"Around the block. Sometimes further if the neighbor's dog isn't out." Eddie set his plate on the crate. "You don't have to do this."

"Do what."

"Check on me. Bring food. Pretend this is normal."

Steve wiped his mouth with a napkin. "It's not pretend."

They sat with that. The TV was on mute in the background, some late-night rerun of a cop show neither of them was watching. Eddie's foot bounced once, then stopped when it pulled on the scar.

"You still at the video store?" Eddie asked.

"Family Video closed last month. I'm at the radio station with Robin now. Nights." Steve looked at his hands. "She asks about you sometimes."

"Tell her I'm not dead."

"I will."

Eddie shifted, trying to find a position that didn't pull. The movement made him wince. Steve was already reaching for the pillow at the other end of the couch before he thought about it. He tucked it under Eddie's knee. Eddie went still.

"You don't have to—"

"I know." Steve sat back. His shoulder brushed Eddie's. Neither of them moved away.

They watched the silent TV. On screen, a detective pointed at a map. The colors were washed out. Steve could hear Eddie breathing, slower now that he wasn't trying to hold himself upright.

"I keep thinking I should go back," Eddie said. Quiet. "Hawkins. See if the trailer is still there. See if Wayne is still mad."

"Wayne isn't mad."

"You don't know that."

"Dustin does. He says Wayne asks about you every time he comes in for a movie." Steve turned his head. Eddie was looking at the TV, not at him. "He wants you to come home."

"Home is a lot of things that almost killed me." Eddie's mouth twitched. "And some things that didn't."

Steve didn't answer. He watched the way Eddie's fingers flexed against his thigh, the way the scar tissue pulled when he did it. He wanted to put his hand over Eddie's. He didn't.

They finished the food. Steve cleaned the plates in the small sink while Eddie stayed on the couch. The water ran hot. He dried them with a dish towel that had a faded logo from some band Steve didn't recognize. When he came back, Eddie had the phone in his good hand.

"Dustin texted again. Says thank you for checking."

"Tell him I'll call him tomorrow."

Eddie typed something. His thumb moved slow. He set the phone down. "You can stay. If you want. Couch is free. I don't sleep much anyway."

Steve looked at the couch. It was short. His legs would hang off the end. "Yeah. Okay."

He sat. Eddie shifted again, careful of the leg, and ended up with his shoulder against Steve's again. The contact was warm through the thin cotton of Eddie's shirt. Steve let his head tip back against the cushion. The cop show had ended. Some talk show was on now, the host laughing at nothing.

"You ever think about what happens after," Eddie said.

"After what."

"After all of it. After we stop running."

Steve closed his eyes. "Sometimes."

"And."

"And I don't know. I keep showing up places I'm not sure I belong." He opened his eyes. Eddie was watching him now. "You?"

"Same." Eddie's voice was low. "Except I keep waiting for someone to tell me to leave. Nobody has yet."

Steve turned his hand over on his knee. Palm up. Eddie looked at it for a long moment. Then he put his good hand in Steve's. The brace made the angle awkward. Steve curled his fingers around Eddie's anyway.

They sat like that until the talk show ended and the late movie started. Eddie's breathing evened out. His head tipped, slow, until it rested against Steve's shoulder. Steve didn't move. He kept his hand where it was and listened to the rain start against the window, the way Eddie had said it would make the leg hurt.

The rain came harder around midnight. Steve woke once when Eddie shifted, a small sound in his throat when the brace caught on the cushion. He didn't pull his hand free. Eddie settled again. Outside, a car passed, headlights sliding across the ceiling. Steve watched the light move and thought about the last time he had been this close to someone without needing to be ready to run. It had been a long time.

In the morning, the leg was stiff. Steve helped Eddie stand. They didn't talk about the hand. Eddie made coffee. Steve found eggs in the fridge that were still good. They ate at the counter because the couch was covered in blankets Eddie had pulled down at some point. The eggs were a little overdone. Eddie ate them anyway.

"You sleep?" Eddie asked.

"Some." Steve rinsed his plate. "You?"

"More than usual." Eddie's mouth did the thing that wasn't quite a smile. "Don't get used to it."

Steve dried his hands. He looked at the scar on Eddie's calf again, the way the skin puckered where the stitches had been. He wanted to ask if it still woke him up. He didn't. "I should get back. Robin has a shift at noon."

Eddie nodded. He walked Steve to the door, slow, one hand on the wall for balance. At the threshold he stopped. The hallway smelled like the neighbor's cooking, something with garlic.

"You can come back," Eddie said. "If you want. Next time Dustin is worried. Or if you're just—whatever."

Steve looked up. Eddie was leaning in the doorway, weight on his good leg, hair in his face. The scar on his calf was visible above the sock. Steve thought about the way Eddie's hand had felt in his. About the rain. About the fact that nobody had told either of them to leave yet.

"Yeah," he said. "I will."

He drove the four hours back with the radio low and the windows cracked. The rain followed him most of the way. When he got home, he texted Dustin that Eddie was fine. Then he sat in the car for a minute longer, hands on the wheel, and let himself think about going back. The station was quiet when he pulled in. Robin was already there, feet up on the desk, reading a magazine. She looked at him when he came in.

"You look like you slept in your car."

"Close enough." Steve hung his jacket. "Eddie says hi."

Robin raised an eyebrow. "Does he."

"Yeah." Steve sat. The chair creaked. He thought about the couch, the weight of Eddie's head on his shoulder, the way the rain had sounded against the window. He didn't say any of that. He just reached for the schedule and started filling in the rest of the week.

Later, when the phones were quiet, he texted Eddie. *Got home. Dustin knows. Leg still okay?*

The reply came after twenty minutes. *Leg is leg. You can come back whenever.*

Steve read it twice. He put the phone face down on the desk. Outside, the rain had stopped. He thought about the next time he would drive north. He didn't set a date. He just left the possibility open, the way Eddie had left the door open when he arrived.
