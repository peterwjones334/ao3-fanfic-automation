The hospital room smelled like antiseptic and the rain that had been falling since they brought him in. Katsuki’s right arm was immobilized from elbow to fingertips, the bandages thick enough that he couldn’t feel the usual heat under his skin. His left hand worked. He flexed it once, small sparks dying against the sheet, and stopped when the motion pulled at the stitches across his ribs.

Izuku sat in the visitor’s chair with his knees drawn up, notebook closed on his lap. He’d changed out of the torn costume into a spare set of clothes someone had brought from the dorms. A line of dried blood still showed at the edge of his hairline where the cut had been cleaned. He hadn’t spoken since the nurses finished the last check.

“You’re not supposed to be in here overnight,” Katsuki said. His voice scraped.

“They said I could stay until morning rounds.” Izuku’s eyes stayed on the floor. “The third car was full of kids from the elementary trip. You—”

“I know what I did.”

The monitor kept its steady count. Katsuki watched Izuku’s fingers tighten and loosen around the notebook edges. The same hands that used to fill pages with notes on his quirk in middle school. The same ones that had come back broken more times than Katsuki could count because Izuku never learned how to stay out of the way.

“The doctor said the nerves in the arm might not come back the way they were,” Izuku said after a while. “If the damage is too deep.”

“They will.” Katsuki shifted. Pain flared sharp enough that he went still again. “Or they won’t. I’m not sitting here either way.”

Izuku looked up. The fluorescent light made the green of his eyes look darker. “You always say that. And then you do something that makes me—” He stopped, mouth pressed flat.

Katsuki waited. Outside the door a cart rolled past, wheels catching on the linoleum.

“Makes you what?”

“Scared.” Izuku’s shoulders dropped. “You make me scared, Kacchan. Every time.”

The word sat in the space between the bed and the chair. Katsuki felt heat climb under his skin that had nothing to do with his quirk. He turned his face toward the window. Rain streaked the glass; the city lights beyond it blurred into soft smears of white and yellow.

“Yeah,” he said. “Same.”

Izuku’s breath caught. Katsuki heard it clearly over the monitor. He didn’t turn back yet. The admission was already out; pulling it in would only make the room smaller. So he kept talking, low, the way he used to when they were kids and no one else was in the room.

“You run in like everything falls apart if you’re not the one holding it. I watch you break yourself open and I don’t know how to make you stop. So I do the other thing. Stand in front. Take the hit. Figure if I’m the one who ends up in the bed, maybe you’ll stay in one place for five minutes.”

The rain hit harder against the window. Izuku didn’t answer right away. Katsuki counted the beeps—twenty-eight—before the chair creaked and Izuku stood. He crossed the short distance and stopped at the edge of the mattress, one hand resting on the rail, careful of the IV line.

“I stay,” he said. Quiet. “Even when you’re not in the bed. You just don’t look.”

Katsuki turned his head. The cut along Izuku’s cheekbone was close enough to see the individual scabs. Freckles showed under the bruising. “I’m looking.”

Izuku’s fingers tightened on the rail. “Then keep looking.”

They stayed like that until the nurse came in for the next round of checks. She adjusted the drip, asked about pain on a scale of one to ten, and didn’t comment on the two of them. Katsuki said four. When she left, the room felt smaller again.

He closed his eyes for a moment. The burn in his arm was steady, but the other thing—the one that had sat under his ribs since the first time Izuku looked at him like he was worth saving—was louder. He didn’t know yet if that was better or worse.

“Deku,” he said.

“Yeah?”

“When they let me out of here, you’re coming to my place. Not the dorms. Mom’s cooking. And you’re not leaving until I say so.”

Izuku’s laugh was small and surprised. “That’s an order?”

“Yeah.” Katsuki opened his eyes. Izuku was smiling, tired and real. “And if you try to sneak out for extra training, I’ll blow up your notebook.”

“You won’t.”

“Try me.”

The next three days blurred into physical therapy that left Katsuki sweating through the thin hospital shirt and checks that measured range of motion he didn’t have yet. Izuku showed up between his own appointments. He brought food that wasn’t from the cafeteria and sat on the floor with his back against the wall, talking about the support course kid who had redesigned the stabilizer gauntlets and the way the city crews were already clearing the collapsed section of track. Katsuki listened more than he answered. When the pain spiked he let Izuku talk him through the breathing count Recovery Girl had taught them years ago, slow and even, until the edges of it dulled.

On the fourth day they discharged him. Mitsuki met them at the curb with the car and didn’t ask questions when Izuku climbed into the back seat with Katsuki’s bag on his lap. At the house she set plates on the table and left them alone with the excuse of laundry. The rice was still warm. Katsuki ate with his left hand. Izuku watched him for a moment, then reached across and adjusted the angle of the chopsticks without asking.

After the dishes were in the sink they ended up on the couch. Izuku’s shoulder rested against Katsuki’s good side, solid and warm through the thin cotton of his shirt. The rain had stopped; the city kept its low, constant hum outside the window.

“I meant what I said,” Katsuki told him. “About being scared.”

“I know.”

“And the other part. About looking.”

Izuku turned his head. Their faces were close enough that Katsuki could count the freckles across the bridge of his nose. “I know that too.”

Katsuki’s good hand found Izuku’s on the cushion between them. He didn’t squeeze hard. The contact was enough. Izuku’s fingers curled back, careful of the fresh bandages still wrapped around his own knuckles from the debris.

“Slow,” Katsuki said. “I’m not good at this.”

“Neither am I.” Izuku’s voice stayed steady. “We can figure it out.”

Katsuki let his head rest against Izuku’s for a second. The weight of it was real and uncomplicated in a way nothing else had been for a long time. The arm still hurt. The rest of him didn’t, not the way it had before.

They stayed until the light outside changed and Mitsuki called from the kitchen that there was leftover curry if they wanted it. Katsuki answered that they did. Izuku didn’t move away. Neither of them mentioned leaving.
