The fluorescent lights in Family Video hummed low enough that Steve could pretend the headache was just from the heat. Outside, the asphalt shimmered. Eddie’s van sat half in the handicap spot, the way it always did when he was waiting for Dustin to finish a shift and didn’t want to admit he was waiting.

Steve flipped a cassette over in his hands. *The Thing*. The cover was cracked. He set it down when the bell rang.

Eddie came in with both hands shoved in his vest pockets, hair damp at the ends like he’d stuck his head under a hose. He didn’t look at the counter right away. He looked at the wall of new releases like the titles might rearrange themselves if he stared hard enough.

“Dustin’s in the back,” Steve said. “Inventory. He’ll be out in ten.”

Eddie nodded. His mouth did that half-smile that never quite reached his eyes anymore. “Cool. I’ll just… browse.”

He drifted toward the horror section. Steve watched the way his shoulders stayed tight under the leather, the way he kept one boot angled toward the door. They had been doing this for weeks—Dustin as the reason, the store as the place—without ever naming it. Steve told himself it was for the kid. Eddie probably told himself the same thing.

The ice machine kicked on again. Eddie flinched.

Steve came around the counter. He didn’t stand too close. “You want a soda or something? We’ve got the good kind in the back.”

Eddie glanced over. One corner of his mouth twitched. “The good kind being the kind that doesn’t taste like metal?”

“The kind that isn’t flat.”

“Bold promise.”

Steve went and got two. He set one on the shelf next to Eddie’s hand. Eddie took it. Their fingers didn’t touch. Steve felt the absence anyway.

They stood there while the machine rattled and the parking lot emptied. Dustin came out eventually, talking fast about a campaign he was planning. Eddie ruffled his hair and called him a nerd. Steve locked up. Outside, the van’s door stuck the way it always did. Eddie kicked it twice before it gave.

“You free tomorrow?” Eddie said, like the words had escaped. He didn’t look at Steve when he said them. “Wayne’s working a double. I was gonna drive out to the quarry. Not—nothing weird. Just air that doesn’t smell like the plant.”

Steve’s hand was still on the store keys. The metal was warm. “Yeah,” he said. “I’m free.”

Eddie nodded once. Got in the van. Drove away with the radio already on, some song Steve didn’t know.

The quarry was quieter than Steve remembered. The water sat flat and dark under the late light. Eddie had parked far enough back that the van was half in shade. He sat on the open tailgate with his boots off, jeans rolled, one foot dangling. A cooler sat between them. Inside were two beers and a bag of ice that was already melting.

“Wayne thinks I’m at the record store,” Eddie said. He cracked a beer and held it out. Steve took it. The can was cold enough to hurt his palm.

They drank. Bugs skimmed the water. Somewhere below them a truck downshifted on the highway.

Eddie spoke first. “You ever think about just… leaving? Not forever. Just until people stop looking at you like you’re gonna start speaking in tongues.”

Steve thought about the way Mrs. Henderson still crossed the street when she saw him. About the way his own parents called once a week now and asked about college like the last year hadn’t happened. “Yeah,” he said. “Sometimes.”

Eddie nodded. He tipped his head back. The light caught the scar that ran under his jaw, thin and pale. Steve had seen it before. He still looked.

“I keep waking up and thinking I’m still—” Eddie stopped. He took a long drink. “Anyway. Air’s better out here.”

They stayed until the light went. Steve drove home with the window down. The radio stayed off. He could still smell the quarry on his shirt. That night he stood under the shower longer than usual and watched the water circle the drain. He thought about the way Eddie’s voice had gone quiet when he talked about leaving. He thought about the scar. He thought about how his hands had stayed still on the steering wheel the whole way back.

The next week Eddie showed up at the store with a box of tapes. “Clearance,” he said. “Wayne found them in the garage. Thought you might want first pick.”

Steve took the box. Inside were mostly horror, a few comedies with the stickers half peeled. He pulled one out. *Evil Dead*. The case was warped from heat.

“You watched this one?” Eddie asked.

“Couple times.”

“Alone?”

Steve shrugged. “Usually.”

Eddie leaned on the counter. Close enough that Steve could see the fray on the cuff of his shirt. “We could watch it. At the trailer. Wayne’s gone till Thursday. If you want. No Dustin. Just—if you want.”

Steve set the tape down carefully. “Yeah,” he said. “Okay.”

The trailer was small. Eddie had cleared the couch and stacked the rest of the tapes on the floor. The VCR clicked when he pressed play. They sat with a careful space between them. On screen, trees moved. Steve’s shoulder brushed Eddie’s once when he shifted. Neither of them moved away.

Halfway through, Eddie’s breathing changed. Steve looked over. Eddie’s hands were fists on his knees. His eyes were open but not tracking the screen.

Steve reached over and paused the tape. The room went quiet except for the fridge.

“Hey,” he said.

Eddie blinked. His mouth opened and closed. “Sorry. Just—sometimes it does that.”

“Want me to turn it off?”

“No.” Eddie’s voice was rough. “Just—talk. For a minute. About anything that isn’t—” He gestured at the dark window.

Steve talked. He talked about the time he and Robin got locked in the store overnight and ate an entire bag of the stale popcorn. He talked about a dog he’d seen behind the gas station that had followed him two blocks before losing interest. He talked until Eddie’s hands unclenched.

When the tape ended, neither of them got up right away. The credits rolled. Eddie’s knee was warm against Steve’s.

“You can stay,” Eddie said. Quiet. “If you want. Couch is fine. Or—” He stopped. “Wayne won’t care.”

Steve stayed. He slept on the couch with his jacket over him. In the morning Eddie made coffee that was too strong and didn’t apologize for it. They ate cereal out of the box. Outside, a neighbor mowed. The sound was ordinary.

Steve left with his hair still damp from Eddie’s shower. He didn’t know when he’d decided to use it. Eddie hadn’t asked. On the drive home he rolled the window down and let the morning air cut through the coffee smell still clinging to his shirt. He stopped at the gas station on the edge of town and bought two coffees he didn’t need. He sat in the parking lot with the engine running and watched people go in and out. None of them looked at him twice. It felt like a small mercy.

They kept meeting. Sometimes at the quarry. Sometimes at the trailer. Once Steve drove them out past the town limits and they sat on the hood of his car and watched heat lightning. Eddie smoked half a cigarette and put it out when Steve coughed. He didn’t light another.

Steve noticed the way Eddie’s hands shook when he thought no one was looking. He noticed the way Eddie watched him when he thought Steve was watching the road. He noticed his own pulse when Eddie laughed at something stupid he’d said. Once, at the quarry, Eddie had leaned over to point at a bird and his hair had brushed Steve’s shoulder. Steve had stayed very still. Eddie had stayed close for three full seconds before he pulled back. Neither of them mentioned it.

One night the power went out at the trailer. Storm. Eddie lit candles because the flashlight batteries were dead. They sat on the floor with their backs against the couch. Rain hit the roof hard enough to drown out the radio.

Eddie was quiet for a long time. Then: “I used to think if I just kept moving it wouldn’t catch up. Like if I stayed loud enough no one would notice the rest.”

Steve looked at the candlelight on Eddie’s hands. “And now?”

“Now I keep waiting for you to get tired of it.” Eddie’s mouth twisted. “Of me. Of the—whatever this is. You don’t have to keep showing up.”

Steve turned. The space between them was small. “I’m not showing up because I have to.”

Eddie looked at him. Really looked. The scar under his jaw caught the light. Steve reached over. He didn’t touch the scar. He touched the back of Eddie’s wrist, where the pulse was jumping.

“I want to,” Steve said. “I keep wanting to.”

Eddie exhaled. It shook. He turned his hand over. Their palms met. Steve’s thumb moved once across Eddie’s knuckles, slow.

Outside, the rain kept going. Inside, the candles burned down. Neither of them let go.

In the morning the power was back. Eddie made coffee. Steve stood at the counter and watched him. When Eddie turned, Steve stepped in. He kissed him once, careful, like he was still asking. Eddie answered by pulling him closer, hands fisting in Steve’s shirt.

They didn’t talk about it for the rest of the day. They didn’t need to. When Steve left, Eddie walked him to the car. He stood in the driveway until Steve pulled out. In the rearview, he was still there, one hand raised, the other in his pocket, like he was holding onto something he hadn’t named yet.

Steve drove with the window down. The air smelled like rain and cut grass. He kept both hands on the wheel and smiled anyway.
