The apartment across the hall had been empty for three weeks when Eddie moved in. Buck noticed because the new neighbor left the door propped open with a cardboard box while he carried things up the stairs, and because the box was labeled CHRISTOPHER in thick marker.

Buck was coming back from a shift, still in his uniform pants and a hoodie, when Eddie set the last box down and straightened. He had a kid on his hip. The boy looked about eight, dark hair falling into his eyes, and he was staring at Buck with open curiosity.

“Need a hand?” Buck asked before he thought about it.

Eddie shook his head. “Almost done. Thanks, though.” His voice was low, a little rough around the edges, like he hadn’t used it much that day. He shifted the kid higher. “I’m Eddie. This is Christopher.”

“Buck,” Buck said. He nodded at the boy. “Hey.”

Christopher waved with the hand that wasn’t wrapped around Eddie’s neck. Eddie gave Buck a small, tight smile and closed the door.

Buck stood in the hallway for a second longer than he needed to, then went inside and put water on for coffee he didn’t really want.

---

They ran into each other in the laundry room two days later. Eddie was folding tiny t-shirts on the folding table while Christopher sat in a plastic chair and colored. Buck had a basket of uniforms and one of the station’s hoodies that always came out smaller than it went in.

“Firefighter?” Eddie asked, nodding at the pile.

“Yeah. Station 118.” Buck dumped detergent in. “You?”

“Army. Was. Medical discharge.” Eddie didn’t look up from the shirts. “Now I’m figuring it out.”

Christopher looked up. “Daddy was a medic.”

“Was,” Eddie said, quiet. He folded another shirt. “You got kids?”

“No.” Buck watched the water start to rise in the machine. “Just a lot of people who treat me like one sometimes.”

Eddie huffed something that might have been a laugh. Christopher went back to his coloring. Buck stayed longer than he needed to, pretending the dryer was louder than it was.

---

The first time Buck saw Eddie hurt was three weeks after that. He was coming up the stairs with groceries when he heard the sound of something heavy hitting the floor in the apartment across from his. He knocked. Eddie opened the door with his left arm cradled against his chest and a tight line between his eyebrows.

“Dropped a box,” he said. “Old injury. It’s fine.”

It wasn’t fine. Buck could see the way Eddie was holding his shoulder, the way his fingers were white at the knuckles. Christopher was on the couch with a book, watching.

“Let me get the rest of it,” Buck said.

Eddie started to refuse, then looked at Christopher and stopped. “Yeah. Okay. Thanks.”

Buck carried the last two boxes inside. The apartment was sparse. A couch, a small table, Christopher’s wheelchair folded against the wall. Eddie sat down carefully on the edge of the couch and didn’t move his arm.

“Ice?” Buck asked.

“In the freezer.” Eddie closed his eyes for a second. “Bottom drawer.”

Buck found the ice pack and wrapped it in a dish towel. When he handed it over, Eddie’s fingers brushed his. Eddie didn’t pull away fast. He just took the pack and settled it against his shoulder.

“Thanks,” he said again.

Buck nodded. He looked at Christopher, who was still watching. “You guys good?”

“Yeah,” Eddie said. His voice was thinner than usual. “We’re good.”

Buck left. He stood in his own apartment with the groceries still in the bags and thought about the way Eddie had looked when he opened the door, like he was calculating how long he could pretend nothing was wrong before someone noticed.

---

After that, Buck started leaving extra food when he cooked. Nothing dramatic. A container of chili with a note that said *too much for one person*. Pasta. Once, a whole tray of roasted vegetables because he’d bought too much zucchini at the market. He never knocked. He just set it on the mat outside Eddie’s door when he knew they were home.

Eddie never said anything about it until the fourth time. Buck was coming home from a night shift, tired enough that the stairs felt longer than usual, and Eddie was standing in the hallway with an empty container in his hand.

“You don’t have to do this,” Eddie said.

“I know.”

“Christopher likes the chili.” Eddie turned the container over in his hands. “He asked if you were coming over.”

Buck felt something catch in his chest. “I can. If you want.”

Eddie looked at him for a long second. Then he stepped back and held the door open. “He’s doing homework. You can help if you want. I’m bad at math.”

Buck went in. Christopher was at the table with a worksheet and a pencil that had teeth marks on it. He looked up and smiled like Buck showing up was normal.

“Buck,” he said. “Do you know fractions?”

“Sometimes,” Buck said. He sat down. Eddie leaned against the counter with his arms crossed, watching them both, and didn’t say much. When Christopher finished the worksheet, Eddie made coffee without asking if Buck wanted any. Buck stayed until the light outside the window went soft and gold.

---

The shoulder got worse before it got better. Buck found out because he heard Eddie swearing, low and continuous, through the wall one night. He knocked. Eddie opened the door with his face pale and his good hand braced on the frame.

“It locked up,” he said. “Can’t get it to move.”

Christopher was asleep on the couch under a blanket. Buck stepped inside and closed the door behind him.

“Sit,” he said.

Eddie sat. Buck stood behind him and put his hands on Eddie’s shoulders, careful, feeling for the knot of muscle that had seized. Eddie went still under his palms.

“Tell me if it hurts,” Buck said.

“It already hurts.”

Buck worked at the muscle until it loosened. Eddie’s breathing went uneven once, then steadied. When Buck stepped back, Eddie rolled the shoulder slowly, testing it.

“Better,” he said. He didn’t turn around. “Thanks.”

Buck washed his hands in the kitchen sink. When he came back, Eddie was still sitting there, looking at the floor.

“You can stay,” Eddie said. “If you want. Couch is free.”

Buck stayed. He slept with his feet hanging off the end of the couch and woke up to Christopher standing over him with a bowl of cereal, asking if Buck wanted some. Eddie was in the kitchen making eggs one-handed. He looked over when Buck sat up and didn’t smile, exactly, but something in his face eased.

---

It went like that for weeks. Buck came over for dinner more nights than he didn’t. Christopher started saving him a seat at the table without being asked. Eddie stopped pretending he didn’t need help when the shoulder acted up. Buck learned the way Eddie liked his coffee and the way he went quiet when he was tired and the way he watched Christopher like the kid was the only solid thing in the room.

One night Buck was late. A call had run long. He texted from the station that he wouldn’t make it. When he got home, there was a note on his door in Eddie’s handwriting. *Christopher made you a plate. Door’s unlocked.*

Buck went across the hall. The apartment was dark except for the light over the stove. Eddie was on the couch with his eyes closed, Christopher asleep against his side. The plate was on the counter, covered in foil.

Buck ate standing up in the kitchen so he wouldn’t wake them. When he was done, he washed the plate and set it in the rack. Eddie opened his eyes as Buck was turning to leave.

“You didn’t have to come over,” Eddie said, voice rough with sleep.

“I know.”

Eddie looked at him. In the low light, his face was all soft edges. “Stay anyway?”

Buck stayed. He sat on the floor with his back against the couch so Eddie wouldn’t have to move. Eddie’s hand found the back of his neck after a while, fingers resting there like he was checking that Buck was still real. Buck closed his eyes and listened to Christopher breathe.

---

The first time they kissed was not dramatic. Buck had come over after a shift that had gone badly. He hadn’t said much. Eddie had put a beer in his hand and sat next to him on the couch while Christopher slept down the hall. They watched half a movie neither of them was paying attention to. At some point Eddie’s knee pressed against Buck’s. Buck turned his head. Eddie was already looking at him.

“Buck,” Eddie said, quiet.

Buck leaned in. Eddie met him halfway. The kiss was careful, like both of them were waiting for the other to pull back. Neither of them did. When they broke apart, Eddie rested his forehead against Buck’s for a second.

“Okay?” he asked.

“Yeah,” Buck said. His voice came out steadier than he felt. “Okay.”

Eddie’s hand stayed on Buck’s knee. They finished the movie like that, shoulders touching, the apartment quiet around them except for the low sound of the television and Christopher turning over in his sleep down the hall.

---

It didn’t fix everything. Eddie still woke up some nights with his shoulder locked and his breathing tight. Buck still came home from shifts with the kind of tired that sat behind his eyes. But the food kept appearing when one of them cooked, and the door stayed unlocked, and Christopher started calling Buck by his name like he had always been there.

One morning Buck woke up on Eddie’s couch again. The light was gray and early. Eddie was in the kitchen in sweatpants and a t-shirt, making coffee one-handed while Christopher sat at the table drawing. Eddie looked over when Buck sat up. He didn’t say anything. He just poured a second cup and set it on the counter where Buck could reach it.

Buck took the cup. The coffee was the way he liked it. Outside, the city was starting to move. Inside, the apartment was warm and quiet and full of the small, ordinary sounds of three people who had figured out how to be in the same room without having to explain why they were there.

Eddie caught Buck looking and raised an eyebrow. Buck shook his head. He drank his coffee. Christopher asked if they could go to the park later. Eddie said yes. Buck said he was off until tomorrow. Eddie nodded like that settled it.

It did.
