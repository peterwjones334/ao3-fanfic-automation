The rink lights were still on when Shane arrived. He had told himself he would only skate for twenty minutes, enough to test the shoulder without anyone watching. The ice was clean. His skates cut the first lines of the night.

Ilya was already there.

He stood at center ice, stick resting across his shoulders, no helmet. The sound of Shane’s entrance made him turn. For a second neither of them moved. Then Ilya skated over, stopping a careful distance away.

“You should not be here,” he said.

“Neither should you.” Shane’s shoulder twinged when he adjusted his grip. “Coach said light skating only.”

“I know what he said.” Ilya’s eyes went to the joint under Shane’s jersey. “You favor the right side.”

Shane started a slow circle. The motion pulled, but it was manageable. “You did not have to come.”

“I wanted to see.” Ilya fell in beside him. Their strides matched without discussion. “The hit was bad.”

“It happens.”

“Not like that.” Ilya’s voice stayed even. “I checked my stick. It was not high.”

Shane kept his eyes on the ice. “I know.”

They had spent years learning each other’s tells. Ilya’s left shoulder dipped before he deked. Shane telegraphed a pass when he was tired. Off the ice the tells were smaller: the way Ilya’s mouth tightened when he was about to say something true, the way Shane went still when he wanted to reach out and did not. Three weeks ago those tells had failed. Or succeeded. Shane still was not sure.

They finished one lap in silence. On the second, Ilya spoke again.

“You are in pain.”

“It is fine.”

“It is not.” Ilya stopped. Shane had to circle back. “Lift your arm.”

Shane did. The range was better than yesterday. Still limited. Ilya watched the motion, then stepped closer. His hand settled on Shane’s upper arm, thumb just below the shoulder seam. The contact was clinical and not. Shane felt the heat through the fabric.

“You ice it after?” Ilya asked.

“Every night.”

“Good.” He did not remove his hand. “The trainer said six weeks. You will ignore that.”

“Probably.”

Ilya’s thumb moved once, a small press. “Do not.”

They stood there until the cold made Shane’s ears ache. When he lowered his arm, Ilya’s hand stayed a moment longer, then dropped. They skated again. This time Ilya stayed on the inside of the curve, close enough that their elbows brushed on the turns. Each contact registered. Shane catalogued them the way he catalogued shifts: time, pressure, what it cost to want more of it.

In the locker room the lights were too bright. Shane sat on the bench and worked his skates off one-handed. Ilya changed at his own stall, back turned. The distance felt deliberate.

“You could have called,” Shane said.

Ilya pulled his shirt over his head. “And said what.”

“That you were coming.”

“I did not know I was coming until I was in the car.” Ilya sat. He had a bruise along his ribs from a different game. It was yellowing at the edges. “Your mother texted me.”

Shane looked up. “She did not.”

“She did. After the game. She said you were not answering her.” Ilya met his eyes. “I told her you were sleeping.”

The lie sat between them. Shane’s mother worried. She had worried more since the trade rumors started and the hits got harder. Ilya had met her once, at a charity event three years ago. She had liked him. Shane had not known what to do with that.

“Thank you,” he said.

Ilya nodded. He reached into his bag and pulled out a small cooler pack. “For the shoulder. Keep it on twenty minutes.”

Shane took it. Their fingers overlapped. Neither pulled away at once.

“Ilya.”

“Do not.” Ilya’s voice was quiet. “Not tonight.”

Shane set the pack against his shoulder. The cold seeped in. “Then when.”

Ilya stood. He zipped his bag. For a moment he looked like he might leave without answering. Then he sat again, closer this time. Their knees touched.

“When you can raise your arm all the way,” he said. “When it does not hurt to do this.” He lifted his own hand and rested it, lightly, against the side of Shane’s neck. His thumb found the pulse point. “Until then we skate. We do not talk about the rest.”

Shane’s breath caught. The touch was careful. He could feel Ilya’s restraint in it, the same restraint that had kept them on opposite sides of every handshake line and every post-game interview for years. He covered Ilya’s wrist with his free hand. Held it there.

“Okay,” he said.

They stayed until the pack warmed and the building settled into night sounds. When they walked out, Ilya walked him to his car. The parking lot was empty except for their two vehicles. Shane unlocked the door, then stopped.

“Same time tomorrow,” he said. It was not a question.

Ilya’s mouth curved, small and real. “If your shoulder allows.”

“It will.”

Ilya stepped back. “Then I will be here.”

Shane got in. He watched Ilya cross to his own car, shoulders loose, hands in his pockets. The ache in Shane’s shoulder was still there. So was the longer ache, the one that had lived under his ribs since the first time they had faced each other across a face-off and both of them had known, without speaking, that the game was only part of it. Tomorrow they would skate again. They would keep the distance they had agreed on. Eventually the distance would end. For now the promise of it was enough.

He started the engine. In the rearview mirror Ilya’s taillights flared, then disappeared into the dark.
