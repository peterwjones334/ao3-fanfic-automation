The airport terminal in Buenos Aires smelled of coffee and jet fuel. Iwaizumi Hajime stood with his duffel bag at his feet, scanning the arrivals crowd for a flash of brown hair and the particular tilt of shoulders he would know anywhere. He had told himself the trip was practical—Oikawa had asked for help reviewing film of his new setter drills, and the club was covering half the flight—but the lie sat poorly in his chest.

Oikawa found him first. He moved through the barrier with the same loose stride that had once carried him across the court at Aoba Johsai, though the edges of it looked sharper now, more deliberate. “Iwa-chan.” The nickname landed soft, almost careful. “You look the same.”

“You don’t,” Iwaizumi said. The words came out flatter than he meant. Oikawa’s hair was longer, the tips bleached lighter by southern sun, and there was a faint scar along his left eyebrow that had not been there at graduation. “Flight was fine. Where’s the car?”

They drove in silence for the first twenty minutes. Oikawa kept both hands on the wheel, posture perfect, the way he drove when he was trying not to fidget. Iwaizumi watched the city slide past and tried not to catalog every small change: the way Oikawa’s mouth stayed closed when he was thinking, the new silver ring on his right index finger, the slight stiffness when he shifted gears.

“You’re quiet,” Oikawa said at last.

“Jet lag.”

“Liar.” A corner of Oikawa’s mouth lifted. “You always talk more when you’re tired. Remember the bus back from Tokyo? You wouldn’t shut up about the convenience store sandwiches.”

Iwaizumi huffed. “Those were bad sandwiches.”

The apartment was on the fourth floor of a building with a cracked tile foyer and a view of a narrow street. Oikawa unlocked the door and stepped aside. “It’s not much. Club housing. I have the bigger room because I’m the setter, apparently.”

The space was tidy in the way of someone who did not spend much time in it. A volleyball sat on the kitchen counter beside an open laptop. Iwaizumi set his bag down and rolled his shoulders. The ache between them had been building for two days of airports.

“Shower’s through there if you want it,” Oikawa said. “I ordered food. It should be here soon.”

Iwaizumi showered. When he came out, the food was on the table—grilled meat, a salad of something green and sharp, bread that was still warm. They ate with their knees almost touching under the small table. Oikawa asked about the training program Iwaizumi had started in Tokyo, about the athletes he worked with, about whether the new athletic tape was any better than the old brand. Iwaizumi answered, and watched the way Oikawa’s fingers worried at the edge of his plate.

After dinner Oikawa pulled up the film. They sat on the couch, laptop balanced between them, and watched set after set of practice. Oikawa narrated in a low voice, pointing out the timing of his approach, the placement of his left foot. Iwaizumi listened and made notes on his phone. It was easy, almost, until Oikawa paused the video on a frame where his form had broken.

“I twisted my ankle last month,” he said. “Nothing serious. Just enough that I had to sit out two games. The coach said it was fine. Everyone said it was fine.”

Iwaizumi turned his head. Oikawa was staring at the screen, jaw tight. “Did you get it looked at properly?”

“Yes. It’s fine now.” Oikawa’s thumb pressed against the trackpad. “I just—when I was on the bench, I kept thinking about how long it would take to get back if it had been worse. How many people were waiting for me to be perfect again.”

The apartment was quiet except for the low hum of the refrigerator. Iwaizumi set his phone down. “You’re allowed to not be perfect.”

“I know that.” Oikawa’s voice was thin. “I just don’t like knowing it.”

They finished the film. Oikawa closed the laptop and set it on the floor. For a moment neither of them moved. Then Oikawa leaned sideways until his shoulder rested against Iwaizumi’s. The contact was light, almost accidental. Iwaizumi stayed still. He could feel the warmth through the thin fabric of Oikawa’s shirt, the slight tremor that ran through the muscle when Oikawa exhaled.

“I missed this,” Oikawa said, so quietly that Iwaizumi almost missed it. “Not the film. Just—being in the same room.”

Iwaizumi closed his eyes. “Yeah.”

They stayed like that until the streetlights outside changed from white to amber. When Oikawa finally stood, he moved carefully, as if the joint between them might break if he pulled away too fast. “You can take the bed. I’ll sleep out here.”

“Don’t be stupid,” Iwaizumi said. “The couch is too short for you.”

Oikawa looked at him for a long moment, then nodded. They brushed their teeth side by side in the small bathroom. Oikawa’s elbow bumped Iwaizumi’s twice. Neither of them mentioned it.

In the dark of the bedroom, Oikawa lay on his back with his hands folded over his stomach. Iwaizumi faced the wall. The mattress dipped between them. After a while Oikawa spoke.

“Do you remember the third year Interhigh? When we lost in the quarterfinals?”

“I remember.”

“I cried in the equipment room. You found me. You didn’t say anything. You just sat on the floor and let me get snot on your jersey.” Oikawa’s voice was steady. “I thought, then, that I would always have that. Someone who would sit with me when I was a mess.”

Iwaizumi turned onto his back. The ceiling was pale in the light from the street. “You still do.”

“Do I?”

The question hung. Iwaizumi could hear his own pulse in his ears. He thought of the years of distance, of the messages that had grown shorter, of the way he had started checking Oikawa’s game schedule before he checked his own. He thought of the way Oikawa had looked at the airport, half-expectant and half-braced.

“Yes,” he said.

Oikawa was quiet for so long that Iwaizumi thought he might have fallen asleep. Then the mattress shifted. Oikawa’s hand found Iwaizumi’s under the blanket, fingers sliding between his, grip careful. Iwaizumi turned his head. In the dim light he could see the line of Oikawa’s profile, the way his eyes were open and fixed on the ceiling.

“I don’t know how to do this if it’s only sometimes,” Oikawa said. “If we only see each other when one of us has a reason.”

“Then we make reasons.” Iwaizumi’s thumb moved once across the back of Oikawa’s hand. “I’m here now. I’m not leaving tomorrow.”

Oikawa’s fingers tightened. “Okay.”

They slept. In the morning Iwaizumi woke to the smell of coffee and the sound of Oikawa moving in the kitchen. He lay still for a moment, listening. When he got up, Oikawa was standing at the counter with two mugs, hair damp from the shower, wearing an old Aoba Johsai shirt that was too big across the shoulders.

“You still have that?” Iwaizumi asked.

Oikawa glanced down at himself, then shrugged. “It fits better than the new ones. They make everything too tight.” He held out a mug. “I looked up flights. There’s one next week that isn’t terrible. If you can stay.”

Iwaizumi took the mug. The coffee was strong and a little bitter. “I can stay.”

Oikawa’s smile was small and real. He set his own mug down and stepped closer, close enough that Iwaizumi could see the scar above his eyebrow clearly. Oikawa’s hand came up, hovered, then settled lightly against the side of Iwaizumi’s neck. His thumb rested just below the jaw.

“Tell me if this is too much,” he said.

“It’s not,” Iwaizumi answered, and leaned in.

The kiss was brief, more breath than pressure. When they parted, Oikawa’s forehead rested against Iwaizumi’s. Outside, a car horn sounded. Somewhere in the building a door closed. Oikawa’s fingers stayed where they were, warm and steady.

They spent the day walking the neighborhood. Oikawa pointed out the bakery that sold medialunas if you arrived before ten, the corner store that stocked the particular brand of sports drink he preferred, the park where he ran when the gym was closed. Iwaizumi listened and matched his stride, noticing the way Oikawa slowed near crosswalks and the way his eyes flicked toward Iwaizumi’s face more often than the path ahead.

At a small café they ordered coffee and sat outside. Oikawa stretched his left ankle under the table, testing the range, then stopped when he caught Iwaizumi watching.

“It’s fine,” he said again.

“I know,” Iwaizumi replied. “I still want to see it.”

Oikawa hesitated, then extended his leg. Iwaizumi crouched and ran careful fingers over the joint, feeling for residual swelling, for the give that meant it was still healing. The skin was warm. Oikawa’s hand rested on the table, knuckles white.

“Does this hurt?” Iwaizumi asked.

“No.” Oikawa’s voice was quiet. “It just feels strange. Having someone else check.”

Iwaizumi stood. “You can tell me when it does.”

They walked back as the afternoon heat built. In the apartment Oikawa showered again while Iwaizumi sorted through the training notes on his laptop. When Oikawa came out, hair wet, he sat on the floor beside the couch and leaned his head against Iwaizumi’s knee. The contact was deliberate this time. Iwaizumi set the laptop aside and rested a hand in Oikawa’s hair, fingers combing through the damp strands.

“The club wants me to stay through the season,” Oikawa said. “Another year, at least. Maybe two.”

“That’s good.”

“Is it?” Oikawa turned his face into Iwaizumi’s palm. “Tokyo is far. Even with flights.”

Iwaizumi’s thumb moved along the line of Oikawa’s temple. “We can figure it out. Video calls. Visits when the schedule allows. You coming back for the off-season.” He paused. “If you want that.”

“I want that,” Oikawa said. The words were muffled against Iwaizumi’s wrist. “I wanted it last year. And the year before. I just didn’t know how to ask without making it sound like I needed you to fix something.”

“You don’t have to need me for that.” Iwaizumi’s hand stayed steady. “You can just want me here.”

Oikawa was quiet. Then he shifted, rising to his knees so they were closer to eye level. His hands settled on Iwaizumi’s thighs, light, waiting. “Okay,” he said. “Then I want you here.”

Iwaizumi leaned forward and kissed him again, slower this time, with the afternoon light falling across the floor between them. When they parted, Oikawa’s eyes were bright and steady. He smiled, small and sure, and rested his forehead against Iwaizumi’s for a second time.

“Okay,” he said, and the word carried more weight than it had that morning. “We’ll figure it out.”

Outside, the street continued its ordinary noise. Inside, the apartment felt less empty than it had the night before. Iwaizumi kept his hand in Oikawa’s hair and stayed exactly where he was.
