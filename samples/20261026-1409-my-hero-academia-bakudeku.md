The light in Katsuki’s kitchen hit the counter at eight. Midoriya knew because he had timed it. He carried the plant across the hall with the pot balanced against his hip and the left arm held close to his side. The leaves had yellowed again overnight. He knocked twice. The door opened before the second knock finished.

Katsuki stood in the doorway in a T-shirt that had not been ironed. His hair was still wet at the ends. He looked at the plant, then at the arm, then at Midoriya’s face.

“Counter,” he said.

Midoriya set the pot where the sun would reach it. Their shoulders brushed when he straightened. Katsuki did not step back. The kitchen smelled of the coffee he had already made and the damp that came off the plant’s soil. Midoriya stayed where he was until the contact ended of its own accord.

“I’ll water it before I leave,” he said.

“You can leave it.”

Katsuki poured a second cup without asking. He set it on the counter next to the pot. Midoriya’s right hand closed around the mug. The left stayed at his side. The scar across the palm showed when he lifted the cup. Katsuki looked at the scar and then at the plant. The leaves were already catching the light.

They had shared the wall for eleven weeks. The lock on Katsuki’s side of the door had been installed the day Midoriya moved in. Midoriya had not asked about it. The file at the agency listed them as a matched pair for structural collapses. The last one had taken the beam across Midoriya’s left side. Katsuki had pulled him clear. The medic had written six weeks. It had been five and a half.

At the agency Midoriya ran the new recruits through the formation drill with the left arm lagging by a half-second. Katsuki watched from the window of the break room. The coffee there was always burnt. He poured a second cup anyway. When Midoriya turned and caught his eye, Katsuki did not look away. The lag was still there. The recruits did not notice. Midoriya corrected a stance and kept moving.

In the locker room after the shift Katsuki found him icing the joint without the wrap. Condensation ran down the forearm and soaked the cuff. Katsuki opened his locker and took the roll of elastic from the shelf. He held it out. Midoriya’s fingers closed around it. The contact lasted the length of a breath. Katsuki did not pull until the bandage was free.

“Wrap it first,” he said.

Midoriya nodded. He worked the bandage even and tight. When he finished he set the ice pack back in the cooler. Katsuki stayed until the room emptied. Midoriya’s shoulder brushed his when he stood. Neither of them spoke. The hall lights clicked to the night cycle as they walked out together.

In the apartment the plant had already been watered. The kitchen light was on. Midoriya stood at the counter with his back to the door, left arm resting on the wood, bandage white against the grain. Katsuki locked the door behind him. The click was the same as every other night. Midoriya turned.

“I can stay on my side,” he said.

“The light’s better here.”

Midoriya’s mouth moved. He did not smile. He looked at the plant, then at Katsuki. The kitchen was small. The space between them was the width of the counter. Katsuki set his keys down. The metal rang once. Midoriya’s left hand stayed on the counter, palm up, scar visible. Katsuki looked at it. He reached past it for the kettle and filled it at the sink. The water ran. Midoriya watched his hands. When the kettle was on the stove Katsuki turned. Midoriya had not moved.

“Tea,” Katsuki said.

“All right.”

They waited. The plant’s leaves moved in the draft from the window. Midoriya’s fingers curled and uncurled against the wood. Katsuki counted the seconds the way he counted distances on a mission. When the kettle whistled he poured. He set one mug in front of Midoriya. Their fingers did not touch. Midoriya lifted the mug with his right hand. The left stayed where it was. Katsuki drank standing. The tea was too hot. He held it anyway.

Outside the building the city lights came on one block at a time. Inside the kitchen the plant stood in the last of the sun. Midoriya set his mug down. The scar faced Katsuki. He did not move his hand. Katsuki looked at the scar and then at the plant and then at Midoriya’s face. He set his own mug down. The space between their hands was the width of the counter’s edge. Neither of them closed it. The kettle clicked off. The light outside the window went blue.

Three days later the arm locked during a drill. Midoriya dropped to one knee on the training floor and stayed there. Katsuki was across the grounds. He crossed the distance at a run. The recruits had already stopped. Midoriya’s left hand was open on the mat, fingers stiff. Katsuki crouched in front of him. He did not touch the arm.

“Range,” he said.

“Half.”

Katsuki nodded. He stayed crouched until Midoriya could stand. They walked off the floor together. In the medic bay Katsuki sat on the edge of the table while the healer worked. Midoriya’s shirt was off. The scar from the beam ran from the shoulder to the elbow, pale and raised. Katsuki looked at it and then at the floor. When the healer left he stayed. Midoriya pulled the shirt back on one-handed. The fabric caught at the bandage.

“You don’t have to wait,” Midoriya said.

“I do.”

They sat until the bay emptied. Katsuki’s hand rested on the table between them, palm down, close enough that Midoriya could have covered it if he moved. He did not. The overhead light hummed. Outside the training grounds the recruits had started the drill again without them.

That night Midoriya did not knock. He came in with the plant already in his arms and set it on the counter. The leaves were greener. Katsuki was at the sink. He turned when the door closed. Midoriya’s left arm was still bandaged. He held the pot with the right. Katsuki dried his hands and crossed the kitchen. He stopped an arm’s length away.

“Show me,” he said.

Midoriya set the plant down. He lifted the hem of the shirt with his right hand. The scar caught the light. Katsuki’s fingers hovered, then settled two inches below the edge of it. The touch was light. It stayed until Midoriya lowered the fabric of his own accord.

“Again tomorrow,” Midoriya said.

Katsuki nodded. His hand dropped. It did not go far. “Yeah.”

They drank the tea standing. The plant stood between them in the last of the sun. The lock on the door stayed open. Outside the window the city lights came on. Inside the kitchen neither of them moved to pour another cup. The kettle clicked off. Midoriya’s left hand rested on the counter, palm up. Katsuki looked at the scar. He did not cover it. He stayed where he was until the light outside went blue and the plant’s leaves no longer moved in the draft.
