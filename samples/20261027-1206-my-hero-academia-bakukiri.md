The training field still smelled like scorched concrete when Bakugou found him.

Kirishima sat on the edge of the bench outside the dorms, left arm cradled against his chest, the hardening quirk having failed halfway through the last drill. The skin along his forearm was mottled red and already swelling. He didn’t look up when Bakugou’s boots stopped in front of him.

“You gonna sit there all night?” Bakugou asked. His voice came out rougher than he meant. The rest of the class had already gone inside; Aizawa had dismissed them early after the third person got clipped by a wayward blast.

Kirishima’s mouth twitched. “Just catching my breath.”

Bakugou crouched. The angle put him low enough to see the way Kirishima’s fingers kept flexing and unflexing, testing the damage. “It’s not broken.”

“I know.”

They stayed like that for a minute. The air between them was still warm from the afternoon, carrying the faint metallic tang of nitroglycerin and dust. Bakugou could hear the others moving around inside—someone laughing, the clatter of plates. Normal sounds. He hated how loud they felt.

“Come on,” he said, standing. “I’ve got the good ice packs.”

Kirishima followed without argument. That was the part that sat wrong in Bakugou’s chest. Kirishima always argued, or at least offered to carry his own weight. Tonight he just walked half a step behind, shoulder brushing the wall when they turned the corner into the common room.

The dorm kitchen was empty. Bakugou pulled the freezer open, shoved aside a bag of frozen peas someone had labeled in Mina’s handwriting, and found the gel packs he kept in the back. He wrapped one in a thin towel without being asked. When he turned, Kirishima was already sitting at the table, arm resting on the wood like he didn’t trust it to stay still.

Bakugou set the pack against the swelling. Kirishima hissed once, then went quiet.

“You don’t have to—” 

“Shut up.” Bakugou adjusted the towel so the cold didn’t sit directly on the worst of the bruise. His fingers stayed a second longer than necessary. “You always do this.”

“Do what?”

“Act like it’s nothing until someone else notices.” Bakugou pulled a chair out and sat across from him, elbows on the table. The distance felt safer. “You hardened wrong. I saw it. You were watching Midoriya’s left side instead of the dummy.”

Kirishima’s ears went pink at the tips. “He was about to take a hit from Todoroki. I figured—”

“You figured you’d tank it.” Bakugou’s jaw worked. “You can’t keep doing that.”

The ice pack shifted. Kirishima steadied it with his free hand, thumb brushing the edge of Bakugou’s towel. Neither of them moved the contact away.

They ate leftover curry because Bakugou didn’t trust Kirishima to stand long enough to cook and because the alternative was watching him pretend he wasn’t hungry. Kirishima managed half a bowl left-handed. Bakugou watched the way his shoulders dropped once the food hit his stomach, the way the line between his brows eased. It was a small thing. Bakugou catalogued it anyway.

After, they ended up on the couch. The common room had emptied; most people had retreated to their rooms or the roof. The television played something with subtitles neither of them read. Kirishima’s arm rested on a pillow between them, the ice pack gone soft and warm. Bakugou’s knee pressed against Kirishima’s thigh through the denim. He told himself it was the angle of the cushions.

“You ever think about after?” Kirishima asked. The question was quiet, aimed at the screen.

Bakugou kept his eyes forward. “After what?”

“All of this. Hero work. The rankings. The way people look at us like we’re already finished.” Kirishima’s voice had gone low the way it did when he was trying not to make something into a speech. “I keep thinking about the people who’ll still be here when the noise dies down. Who’ll still answer if I call at two in the morning because something went wrong.”

Bakugou’s hands stayed loose on his thighs. “You’re not dying tomorrow.”

“I know.” A pause. “But I want to know who I’d call.”

The words landed and stayed. Bakugou felt them under his ribs, the same place the explosion always sat when he held it too long. He could name the people Kirishima would call. He could name the one he wouldn’t say out loud.

“You’d call me,” Bakugou said. It wasn’t a question.

Kirishima turned his head. In the low light from the kitchen, the red of his hair looked darker, almost brown at the roots. “Yeah. I would.”

They didn’t talk after that. The show ended. Someone’s alarm went off upstairs and was silenced. Kirishima’s breathing evened out; his head tipped until it rested against Bakugou’s shoulder. The weight was familiar in a way that made Bakugou’s pulse jump. He didn’t shift. He counted the seconds between Kirishima’s exhales and matched them, the way he’d learned to do on long stakeouts and longer nights after bad calls.

When the arm finally slipped, Bakugou caught it before it hit the cushion. Kirishima made a small sound in his sleep—half protest, half relief—and settled again. Bakugou kept his hand where it was, palm against the warm skin just above the bruise, and watched the dark outside the windows turn from blue to black.

He stayed until the ice pack had melted completely and the common room clock ticked past midnight. Then he stood carefully, slid one arm under Kirishima’s knees and the other behind his back, and carried him the short distance to the elevator. Kirishima’s head lolled against Bakugou’s collarbone. He smelled like the soap they all used and the faint ozone of his quirk.

In the hallway outside their rooms, Bakugou hesitated. Kirishima’s door was closer. He pushed it open with his shoulder and set him on the bed, boots still on. The room was the same as always: dumbbells under the desk, a poster of some pro hero Bakugou pretended not to recognize, a stack of notes from class. Ordinary. Bakugou pulled the blanket up over Kirishima’s legs and turned the desk lamp off.

He was at the door when Kirishima spoke.

“Stay.”

The word was thick with sleep. Bakugou’s hand stayed on the frame.

“Your arm—”

“It’s fine. Just—stay. For a minute.”

Bakugou closed the door. He sat on the edge of the bed, back to the wall, and listened to Kirishima’s breathing settle again. Outside, the wind moved across the training field and made the metal bleachers creak. Inside, the only sound was the low hum of the building and the occasional shift of fabric when Kirishima adjusted his arm.

An hour passed. Maybe two. Bakugou’s eyes had closed at some point. He opened them when the mattress dipped and Kirishima’s uninjured hand found his wrist in the dark.

“You didn’t leave,” Kirishima said.

“You asked.”

Kirishima’s thumb moved once, slow, over the pulse point. “I keep thinking you’re going to get tired of it. Of me needing things.”

Bakugou turned his hand so their palms met. The contact was small. It still felt like standing too close to an open flame.

“I’m not tired,” he said. “And you’re not a thing that needs. You’re just—here.”

Kirishima’s fingers tightened. In the dark, Bakugou could see the outline of his smile, the one that usually came with teeth and a challenge. This one was smaller.

“Okay,” Kirishima said. “Then stay.”

Bakugou stayed. He stayed while the sky outside the window lightened by degrees and while Kirishima’s breathing stayed steady against his side. When the first alarm went off down the hall, he didn’t move. Kirishima’s hand was still in his, warm and solid, and the bruise on his arm had already started to fade under the careful pressure of the night.

They would have to get up. They would have to pretend, for a little while longer, that nothing had shifted. But the distance between their rooms felt shorter than it had the day before, and Bakugou thought—quietly, the way he kept most things—that he could live with that.

He squeezed once, just enough to be felt, and let his eyes close again.

---

The morning came with the usual noise: doors opening, someone yelling about missing socks, the smell of coffee from the kitchen. Bakugou woke with his neck stiff and Kirishima’s hair in his mouth. He didn’t pull away immediately. Kirishima was already awake, watching him with the careful stillness of someone who expected the moment to break.

“Morning,” Kirishima said. His voice was rough.

Bakugou sat up. The blanket had twisted around both of them sometime in the night. “Your arm.”

Kirishima flexed it. The swelling had gone down. “Better. You didn’t have to—”

“I know.” Bakugou stood. His boots were still by the door. He bent to pull them on, then stopped. “You want breakfast?”

Kirishima blinked. “You’re cooking?”

“I’m not letting you burn the kitchen down left-handed.” Bakugou opened the door. The hallway was bright. He looked back once. “Come on. Before Midoriya eats all the eggs.”

They walked to the kitchen together. Kirishima’s shoulder brushed Bakugou’s once, deliberate. Neither of them mentioned it. At the stove, Bakugou cracked eggs into a pan and watched the edges set. Kirishima stood at the counter, cutting fruit with his right hand, humming under his breath the way he did when he was trying not to smile too wide.

When Bakugou slid a plate across the table, their fingers touched. Kirishima didn’t pull away. Bakugou didn’t either.

“Thanks,” Kirishima said.

Bakugou nodded. He sat, and for the first time in weeks the space between them felt like something they had both chosen.

Outside, the training field was already filling with the sound of people starting their day. Inside, the kitchen was warm, and the bruise on Kirishima’s arm was fading, and Bakugou thought he could get used to the quiet that came after the noise.

He picked up his fork. Kirishima’s knee rested against his under the table. Neither of them moved it.

They ate.

---

Later, when the day had worn itself thin and the dorms had gone quiet again, Bakugou found Kirishima on the roof. The city lights were low on the horizon. Kirishima’s arm was bare now, the skin still faintly marked but no longer swollen.

“You always come up here when you can’t sleep,” Bakugou said.

Kirishima glanced over. “You always know where I am.”

Bakugou sat beside him, close enough that their shoulders touched. The concrete was cold through his jeans. He didn’t mind.

“I meant it,” he said. “About calling. And about not being tired.”

Kirishima was quiet for a long moment. Then he turned, and the look on his face was open in a way Bakugou had only seen a handful of times—after hard wins, after harder losses, in the seconds before something important.

“I know,” Kirishima said. “I’ve known for a while. I just didn’t want to make it harder for you.”

Bakugou’s laugh was short. “You’re an idiot.”

“Yeah.” Kirishima’s mouth curved. “But I’m your idiot, if you want.”

The words sat between them, simple and heavy. Bakugou looked at him—the red hair catching the last of the light, the careful way he held himself, the steadiness that had never once asked Bakugou to be less than he was.

He reached out. His hand settled at the back of Kirishima’s neck, thumb brushing the short hair there. Kirishima leaned into it without hesitation.

“I want,” Bakugou said.

The kiss was slow. Careful at first, then less so when Kirishima’s uninjured hand found the front of Bakugou’s shirt and held on. It tasted like the fruit from breakfast and the night air and something Bakugou had been holding behind his teeth for months. When they parted, Kirishima’s forehead rested against his.

“Stay,” Kirishima said again, quieter this time.

Bakugou nodded. “Yeah. I’m staying.”

They sat on the roof until the lights in the dorms started to go out one by one. Kirishima’s hand stayed in his. The city kept moving below them, loud and distant, and the space between their rooms no longer felt like distance at all.
